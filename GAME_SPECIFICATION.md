# Game Design & Content Specification: Planetary Scale RTS

**Companion Document to:** [`SPECIFICATION.md`](SPECIFICATION.md) (Engine Technical Specification)  
**Target Engine:** Deterministic Fixed-Point Simulation Core (`sim-engine`)

---

## 1. Executive Vision & Core Pillars

### 1.1 The Vision
A grand-scale, flow-economy real-time strategy game. Players command planetary armies ranging from light scouts to colossal multi-screen Experimentals, orchestrating warfare across land, sea, subsurface, and air on maps spanning up to 81×81 km (6,500+ sq km).

**The setting.** Colonies were planted far beyond any supply line, so each came with a garrison that could build itself: at its heart, a command rig carrying the design library and fabrication gear for a whole army, able to raise factories from local rock and sunlight. On the frontier world of Halvard, the colonial government split over its founding charter, and the garrison, the **Frontier Garrison Corps**, split with it. The **Charter** holds to the colony's founding law; the **Accord** broke away to write a new one. Both halves fight with the same doctrine, the same designs, and the same rigs: the war is decided by commanders, not technology. The tone is grounded and military: functional names, olive and grey machines in team markings, and no mysticism.

### 1.2 Core Pillars
1. **The Strategic Zoom:** No artificial camera boundaries. The perspective pans and zooms continuously from close-ups just above the ground out to a theater view where units collapse into standardized NATO icons.
2. **The Flow-Based Economy:** An economy governed by continuous streaming rates rather than static stockpiles. Players balance resource inflow versus drain in real-time differential equations.
3. **True Physical Ballistics:** Every bullet, missile, and artillery shell exists as a simulated 3D physical object governed by mass, velocity, gravity, and terrain collision.
4. **One Army, Then Doctrines:** Both sides start as mirror images, the Charter and the Accord fielding the same roster, so a match is decided by play. A second faction comes later, set apart by what its roster emphasizes within the same mechanics (few expensive units against many cheap ones, early strength against late, range against mobility), not by new systems (§14).
5. **Mass-Scale Wreckage Recycling:** War is funded by the graveyard of past battles. Debris is physical, harvestable, and strategically contested.

## 2. The Command Units (Command Rig & Field Rigs)

### 2.1 The Command Rig
The player’s physical avatar on the battlefield. The Command Rig is a multi-story walker: brawler, primary engineer, and high-yield resource generator.

* **Assassination Condition:** Under standard match rules, when a commander’s health reaches 0, its reactor fails in a massive explosion (dealing ~15,000 damage in a 25-meter radius), destroying surrounding bases and instantly eliminating the player from the match.
* **Discharge:**
  * Dumps a substantial pulse of the rig's stored Energy into one shot.
  * Destroys virtually any Tier 1, 2, or 3 mobile unit it hits.
  * Ineffective against Tier 4 Experimentals and structures (deals capped damage).
* **Field Enhancements:**
  The rig has three bays, each holding one enhancement:
  * **Weapons:** a heavier cannon with more range and damage.
  * **Fabrication:** the T2 and T3 Fabrication Packs, which add build power and the higher tiers' structures.
  * **Systems:** protection, sensors, repair, and the rest: Field Shield and Area Shield, Signature Suppression and then Thermal Camouflage, Damage Control, Orbital Redeploy, and the Onboard Reactor.
* **Orbital Redeploy:**
  * A rig with it can be redeployed anywhere on the map within sensor coverage, flown up by dropship and set down at a beacon.
  * *Mechanics:* Requires charging a large buffer of stored Energy first. If energy stalls during the charge, the redeploy aborts. Landing on enemy units destroys them.
* **Self-Destruct (Ctrl+K):** Initiates a 5-second countdown leading to an intentional reactor detonation. Used to deny enemy reclaim, to take an enemy base down with the rig, or to free up unit capacity.

**Status:** Field enhancements in all three bays, Orbital Redeploy, and self-destruct are built (§18.4, Field Enhancements).

### 2.2 Field Rigs
Constructed in the late game at the **Rig Yard (Tier 3)**:
* Function as autonomous sub-commanders with high baseline Build Power and heavy armor.
* Do not end the game upon death (no game-ending reactor detonation).
* Have bays of their own, with their own enhancements: Long Barrel and Capacitor Bank for weapons, the Rapid Fabricator, and shields, a Radar Jammer, a Sensor Mast, Orbital Redeploy, and an Onboard Reactor for systems.

**Status:** Built (§18.4, Field Rigs). Ready-made loadouts from the Rig Yard are not.

## 3. The Streaming Economy

The game operates on a continuous rate-of-flow system measured in **Resource / Second**.

```
Net Materiel Rate   = Total Materiel Inflow - Total Materiel Outflow
Net Energy Rate = Total Energy Inflow - Total Energy Outflow
```

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      RESOURCE PIPELINE                      │
│                                                             │
│   INFLOW SOURCES                    DRAIN CONSUMERS         │
│   • Materiel Extractors (Mexes)         • Factories / Upgrades  │
│   • Power Generators (PGens)        • Engineers (Assisting) │
│   • Geothermal Vents               • Shield Dome Upkeep    │
│   • Materiel Fabricators (Energy->Materiel) • Radar / Deep scan Upkeep   │
│   • Battlefield Reclaim             • Weapons (Artillery)   │
│                                                             │
│             ▲                               ▲               │
│             │                               │               │
│             └───────► [ STORAGE POOL ] ─────┘               │
│                       (Buffer Overflow)                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3.1 Resource Types
1. **Materiel:** The structural matter used to construct physical chassis, weapons, and buildings. Gathered from fixed Materiel deposits, battlefield reclaim, or energy conversion.
2. **Energy:** The operational fuel used to power shields, sensors, weapons, construction nanite streams, and materiel fabricators. Gathered from Power Generators and Geothermal Plants.

### 3.2 Storage Buffers & Stalling Penalties
Small baseline storage pools buffer transient rate spikes. When storage hits zero and consumption exceeds production, the empire **stalls**:

* **Materiel Stall:** When Materiel hits 0, all active construction, factory queues, and upgrades slow down proportionally to match incoming revenue:
  $$\text{Progress Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Inflow}}{\text{Total Demand}}$$
* **Energy Stall:** When Energy hits 0, the consequences are immediate and catastrophic:
  * Factory and engineering build rates slow to a near halt.
  * Radar, Sonar, and Deep scan sensors go blind (blinking off).
  * Active energy shield domes rapidly bleed power and **completely collapse**, leaving bases defenseless.
* **Production Pausing:** Individual factories, shields, materiel fabricators, and construction projects can be manually paused (hotkey toggle) to instantly cut their drain from the network without canceling queued progress.

### 3.3 Materiel Fabrication (The Post-Scarcity Transition)
* **Tier 1 & Tier 3 Materiel Fabricators:** Stationary structures that continuously convert excess Energy into raw Materiel.
* Highly volatile: Explode violently if destroyed while active.
* Allows economies to scale infinitely beyond the physical materiel points on the map.

**Status:** Built (§18.4, Materiel Fabrication and Geothermal Power), as T2 and T3 fabricators.

### 3.4 Geothermal Plants
Placed on specialized map vents. Provide high early-game energy output (equivalent to ~10–12 T1 power generators) at a fraction of the build cost, creating high-value early-game territorial objectives.

**Status:** Built (§18.4, Materiel Fabrication and Geothermal Power), with the output of 5 generators.

## 4. Base Geometry & Adjacency Networks

Structures built directly touching one another share physical power and resource connections, providing passive efficiency bonuses.

```
   [PGen]                  [Storage]
     │                         │
[Factory] ◄── Energy Discount [Mex] ◄── +12.5% Materiel Boost
     │                         │
   [PGen]                  [Storage]
```

* **Factory + Power Generators:** Reduces the continuous Energy cost of unit production by up to 25%.
* **Factory + Materiel Storage / Materiel Fabricator:** Reduces the Materiel cost of unit construction by up to 15%.
* **Materiel Extractor + Materiel Storage:** Surrounding a Materiel Extractor with Materiel Storage buildings on all four cardinal edges increases extractor output by **+50%** (+12.5% per storage unit).
* **Power Generator + Energy Shields / Radar:** Reduces the continuous operational Energy upkeep by up to 25%.
* **Power Generator + Heavy Artillery:** Reduces Energy drain per shot and **decreases reload times**, directly increasing the artillery's rate of fire.
* **Volatility Hazard:** Higher-tier generators (T3 PGens) and materiel fabricators contain unstable plasma cores. If destroyed, they explode with devastating kinetic force, triggering chain reactions that can vaporize adjacent base structures.

**Status:** Built with a different adjacency table from the numbers above (§18.4, Adjacency), so that extractors, not storage, cut a factory's materiel cost. Generators beside artillery installations also cut what each shot costs and the time between shots (§18.4, Artillery).

## 5. Engineering Dynamics & Production Scaling

### 5.1 Build Power (BP) & The Assisting Mechanic
Every constructor (commander, Engineer, Factory, Field Rig) has a distinct **Build Power** attribute.
* **Factory Assisting:** Instead of building 20 separate factories, players can command 40 engineers to "Assist" a single factory.
* The factory's production queue drains the economy at the pooled speed of all 41 units combined, printing high-tier units or Experimentals in seconds.
* **Upgrade Vulnerability:** Upgrading a factory or Materiel Extractor to the next tech tier temporarily halts its unit production or resource generation until the upgrade finishes.

### 5.2 Factory Automation & Infinite Loops
* **Repeat Mode:** Factories can toggle infinite build queue looping (e.g., repeating a strike group of 5 tanks, 2 flak, and 1 mobile missile).
* **Dynamic Rally Orders:** Rally points can be anchored to friendly units (auto-guard), transport aircraft (auto-ferry), or other factories (auto-assist upon completion).

### 5.3 The Capture Mechanic (Nanite Subversion)
* Engineers, commanders, and field rigs can fire capture beams onto enemy structures or mobile units.
* If completed without the engineer dying or the target being destroyed, ownership of the asset permanently transfers to the capturing player.
* Allows capturing enemy technology to unlock opposing faction tech trees (e.g., building another faction's stealth fields).

**Status:** Built (§18.4, Capture). Unlocking another faction's tech waits for factions (§14).

## 6. The Wreckage Economy & Denial Tactics

### 6.1 Battlefield Reclamation
Destroyed units, downed aircraft, shattered base buildings, rocks, and trees leave physical 3D wreckage on the terrain.
* **Materiel Recovery:** A wreck keeps 90% of the unit's materiel cost, times 90% at T1 (and for commanders), 80% at T2, 70% at T3, or 60% for experimentals, so 81%, 72%, or 63% of it, and 60% of that again if it sank, from a unit that died over water: a T3 battleship that sinks leaves 3,780 of its 10,000. A wreck has 90% of the unit's health. A unit less than half built leaves nothing, nor does a structure that was not finished.
* **Wrecks at Sea and Downed Aircraft:** A ship's wreck sinks to the sea floor. A downed aircraft's wreck keeps going the way it flew as it falls, onto the land or into the sea, where it sinks too. A wreck on the sea floor can still be reclaimed, by an engineer floating on the water over it (§18.4, Sinking and Falling Wrecks).
* **Graveyard Flipping:** After a major mid-map clash, the victor can move mobile engineers or commanders into the graveyard to vacuum up thousands of Materiel units in moments, immediately funding higher-tier tech or Experimentals.
* **Environmental Reclaim:** Trees provide fast early Energy (and minor Materiel); rocks provide pure, dense early Materiel.

### 6.2 Wreckage Blasting (Denial of Reclaim)
* Wreckage possesses its own collision box and hit-point pool.
* Players can command tanks or artillery to **Force-Fire (Ground Attack)** on wreckage graveyards.
* Explosive damage vaporizes the wreckage, permanently deleting the materiel from the map to prevent the enemy from reclaiming it.

**Status:** Built (§18.4, Wreck Blasting). Debris is hit where shells come down and where blasts reach, not along a shell's path.

## 7. Physics, Ballistics & Weapon Trajectories

### 7.1 Ballistic Simulation
* **True Trajectory:** Shells, plasma charges, missiles, and rockets travel with real muzzle velocity and gravitational drop.
* **Elevation & Masking:** Cliffs, hills, and base walls physically block low-trajectory fire. Artillery placed on ridges gains increased line-of-sight and clearance.
* **Physical Dodging:** Agile units (light walkers, interceptors) can change heading to evade incoming slow-moving artillery shells or unguided rockets.
* **Guns and Line of Fire:** Every weapon fires from the top of its unit, not its middle, so that a ship fires over the bank above it and a tank over a low rise. A gun on a turret of its own, as each of the battleship's three is, aims and fires from where its turret sits on the hull, and a turret aft rests facing the stern. A new shell is drawn leaving its gun, though by the time it is first seen it has flown a tick. A shell's blast is centred where it strikes, on a unit or the ground, and the renderer is told that point, so that a shell is drawn all the way there and bursts there as it arrives: drawn from its last position, a tick short of what it hit, every shell used to look as though it landed short. A shell is drawn by the weapon that fired it (2026-11-24): a thin white tracer for a gun with no blast, an orange tracer for a direct-fire shell with one, and for artillery, a battleship's main guns, bombs, and flak a dark round with a glowing base, bigger at each tier, artillery and bombs trailing a wisp of smoke; a torpedo under the water shows only as bubbles on the surface over it. Every one collapses to a small dot when zoomed far out. A unit told to attack a target, or a spot, that the ground hides from its gun does not shell the ground in the way: it walks straight at it, or by its route, until the shot is clear. One that can get no closer, for two seconds without moving, or because there is no way there, fires anyway, from where it is, until it moves again. Artillery fires over most of what is in the way, and does not walk closer. A ground attack's shot must land within 8, or its blast, of the spot. An attack also stops closing in once any of the unit's weapons is firing at the target: a submarine's deck gun at a unit on land that its torpedoes cannot reach.
* **Leading Shots:** Every shell aims where its target will be when it arrives: the first point along the target's course that the shell can reach by the time the target gets there, and it takes just that long, flying a little slower than it could if that is what it takes not to arrive early. A unit moving steadily, across the line of fire, straight at the gun or straight away, is hit; only a change of direction after the shot dodges it. Artillery leads too, but its shells fly for 2 seconds or more and scatter, so changing direction, spreading out against its splash, and closing in beat it: massed tanks against massed artillery is meant to turn on that micro (BALANCE.md). Bombs lead too: a bomber lets go when its bomb, keeping the bomber's speed as it falls, will land where the target will be by then, and steers for that point. Only anti-air missiles home: the SAM's and the cruiser's steer themselves to their aircraft, as torpedoes do to ships. Every other shot at an aircraft, a gun's or flak's, flies straight, in three dimensions, to where the aircraft will be when it can first get there if it holds its course: aiming along the aircraft's turn as well as its course, from how far it has been turning a tick over the last few: a gun's shell hits the nearest aircraft within 24 of its edge there, for a gun fires a burst of rounds, so that an aircraft flying straight or turning steadily is hit and one that changes what it is doing may not be, and flak bursts there, hitting every enemy aircraft within its blast, and the faster the aircraft and the longer the flight, the wider it scatters (§10, Anti-Air by Tech Level). Until 2026-11-18 every shot at an aircraft homed and none could miss. Cruise missiles neither lead nor follow: they fly to where the target was when they were launched, since their long range would make them far too strong against moving targets otherwise. Until 2026-11-19 the aim was refined only twice from where the target was, which fell short of a target coming on or going away from slow shells (by about a tank's width at light artillery's speed, by far more at slower ones). Before that, shells aimed where the target was, so a tank driving across a gun's front at full speed walked out from under every shell, and a blob walking straight at artillery dodged much of it without trying.
* **Friendly Fire:** Stray rounds, premature detonations, and artillery spread inflict full damage on friendly units and shields.
* **Beam Weapons:** Continuous raycast energy vectors (e.g., microwave lasers) apply damage instantly along line-of-sight without projectile flight delays.

## 8. Multi-Layered Defense Networks

Static defenses must be deployed in coordinated layers to resist high-tier assaults:

| Defense System | Ordnance Targeted | Core Mechanics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Point Defense (PD)** | Direct ground fire | High DPS direct-fire turrets (Gauss, pulse lasers). Blocked by terrain. |
| **Anti-Air (AA)** | Aircraft (Interceptors, Gunships, Bombers) | **Flak:** High AoE burst against light clusters.<br>**SAMs:** High-damage tracking missiles targeting heavy air. |
| **Energy Shields** | All ballistic & explosive shells | Projects a 3D hemispherical dome with its own HP pool. Absorbs incoming fire until depleted. Overlapping shields suffer AoE splash bleed-through. |
| **Tactical Missiles & TMD** | Ground installations, shields | **Missiles:** High-burst cruise missiles designed to collapse shields.<br>**TMD:** Precision lasers/counter-missiles shooting down incoming warheads in flight. |
| **Strategic Nukes & SMD** | Map-wide base obliteration | **ICBM:** Enormous nuclear warhead wiping out entire bases.<br>**SMD:** Strategic interceptor silos that must manually build and store interceptor missiles. |

### 8.1 Shield Mechanics & Stacking Rules
* **Directional Shield Permeability:** Friendly units inside a shield bubble can freely fire outward. Hostile projectiles coming from outside are blocked by the dome.
* **Shield Overlap Hazard:** Overlapping multiple shield generators does not create invulnerability. Explosive area-of-effect (AoE) shells that strike the outer shield will bleed splash damage into all overlapping inner shield generators simultaneously.

**Status:** Shields, tactical missiles, nuclear missiles, and both missile defenses are built (§18.4, Shields and Missiles). Point defense was already in the slice. Anti-air is built (§18.4, Aircraft, and T2 and T3 Aircraft): the T1 turret and mobile anti-air, T2 flak, which bursts among aircraft, and the T3 SAM launcher.

## 9. Information Warfare (Sensors & Intel)

Combat occurs far beyond visible optical range. Controlling the intel war dictates weapon accuracy and target acquisition.

Fog of war is on by default. A match can be set up without it (`--fog off`, or Fog of war: Off on the browser's setup page), and then every team, the AI included, sees the whole map, and radar, sonar, stealth, and cloaking have nothing to hide from.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     THE INTEL SPECTRUM                      │
│                                                             │
│  [ OPTICAL LOS ]  Reveals unit models & direct sight lines  │
│         ▲                                                   │
│  [ RADAR / SONAR ] Identifies targets as generic blips      │
│         ▲                                                   │
│  [ RADAR STEALTH ] Masks unit blips from radar/sonar        │
│         ▲                                                   │
│  [ OPTICAL CLOAK ] Complete visual & radar invisibility     │
│         ▲                                                   │
│  [ OMNI SENSORS ] Pierces Stealth, Cloak, and Jamming       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

* **Line of Sight (LOS):** Direct optical vision. Only units within direct LOS reveal their 3D models and exact health bars.
* **Radar & Sonar:** Identifies surface/air and subsurface targets as generic triangular blips through the Fog of War.
* **Radar Stealth:** Eliminates a unit's radar signature (it produces no blip), but remains visible to optical eyesight or direct scouts.
* **Optical Cloaking:** Completely conceals the unit visually and electronically. Requires proximity or deep scan to reveal.
* **Radar Jamming:** Deploys dozens of false decoy blips on enemy radar screens to mask true troop numbers and trigger false artillery bombardment.
* **Deep Scan Arrays:** Built into every Command Rig and Field Rig, and built as Tier 3 Deep Scan Array towers. Strips away Stealth, Cloaking, and Jamming within its spherical detection radius.
* **Remote Optics (T3, later):** A facility that drains continuous high Energy to look at a chosen spot anywhere on the map through the Fog of War.
* **Remembered Structures:** Structures do not move, so an enemy structure a team has seen once stays on its map, faded, as it was when last seen: its health, construction, and facing do not update until the team sees it again, and structures built since do not show until they are found. One found only on radar stays as an unidentified structure contact where it stands, its type and health unknown, until something sees it. Either can be fired at as if seen: units in range pick it on their own, and an order can attack it, so that artillery can shell a position a scout has spotted once. One destroyed or captured out of sight stays on the map until the team sees the spot; units stop picking it on their own, as there is nothing there to shoot, and an order to attack it fires at where it was. The AI remembers the structures it has seen the same way.


**Status:** Radar, radar stealth, jamming, deep scan arrays, and optical cloaking are built (§18.4, Intel). Sonar waits for naval units (§10), and the remote optics for T3 factions (§14).

## 10. Multi-Domain Warfare (Air, Sea & Subsurface)

* **Aviation Logistics & Fuel:** Aircraft utilize fuel reserves during continuous flight. Exhausted fuel causes aircraft to lose altitude and crawl at minimum speed. Players build **Airfields** where aircraft land to rapidly refuel, rearm, and repair.
* **Anti-Air by Tech Level:** T3 aircraft other than gunships and transports are tough and fast enough that lesser anti-air does little to them. T1 anti-air does too little damage: as much materiel of it as the aircraft cost cannot bring one down in a pass, nor a strategic bomber over 30 seconds of attack. T2 flak (the Flak Cannon and Mobile Flak) fires slow shells, 40 a tick, that fly straight to where the aircraft will be if it holds its course and burst there, scattering by their weapon's spread (40 for the cannon, 60 for Mobile Flak) and an eighth of how far the aircraft flies while the shell is in the air. Gunships and transports, which hover over their work or fly slowly, are caught; a fast aircraft that turns, as fighters and scouts always are, is not, and even one flying straight is often missed: as much flak as the aircraft cost takes under half its health, though three times as much all but brings down a strategic bomber attacking it. The SAM Launcher is the deterrent: one takes over half a strategic bomber's health in a pass, and two bring it down. The cruiser, the fleet's anti-air, has done more than a SAM since 2026-11-25: its anti-air fires eight missiles of 200 over a second, a volley every two seconds, and the volleys it takes to destroy each aircraft are set (`[volleys]`), four for the Strategic Bomber, where a SAM, 850 every 1.4 seconds, takes about 11 seconds (`[kill_times]`). It did about three quarters of what a SAM does until then. To make this so (2026-11-18), the Air Superiority Fighter went to 4,500 health and 50 a tick, the Strategic Bomber to 7,500 and 40, and the Spy Plane to 2,000; the SAM to 2,400 damage every 3.2 seconds, and the cruiser's anti-air to 1,100 every 2. Before, every anti-air shot homed and none could miss, so speed protected nothing; now only the SAM's and the cruiser's missiles do. `tests/anti_air_test.rs` measures each anti-air against each aircraft, flying over, circling, and attacking, prints the table with `--nocapture`, and checks these bounds. The playtest sandbox has a range of Red anti-air to fly over.
* **Flight and Dogfights (2026-11-22):** Aircraft fly at heights of their own, as shares of the spy plane's, the highest: fighters and heavy bombers at 80%, the air scout at 70%, T1 bombers and interceptors at 60%, gunships and transports at 45%. Anti-air reach counts how far up an aircraft is, and an anti-air gun's shells, like flak, scatter by a fifth of how far the aircraft flies while they are in the air, so T1 anti-air nearly always hits a T1 bomber, mostly hits an interceptor, and next to never a spy plane; homing missiles always hit. Fighters turn half again as fast as the T1 bomber, and the T3 fighter a quarter faster again than the interceptor; it outclasses it, with ten times its damage a second, a shot that destroys one, and more range and speed, and its guns fire off its nose, so that it can take on a swarm. An interceptor's guns are fixed to its nose and fire only within 30 degrees of straight ahead, and in range behind a slower aircraft it eases off to that aircraft's speed to stay on its tail. A bomber that would come round before its bomb is ready flies further out first, and keeps a straight run at its target however tight its turn, so it never wastes a pass. Out of fuel, a bomber flies low and slow and still bombs.
* **Subsurface Warfare:** Submarines and underwater structures are immune to surface artillery, plasma cannons, and direct lasers. Combating them requires dedicated Sonar networks, Torpedo Bombers, Sub-hunters, and Naval Depth Charges.
* **Amphibious Dynamics:**
  * **Seabed Walkers:** Heavy tanks and assault mechs walk along the ocean floor, immune to surface fire but vulnerable to torpedoes and depth charges. Every sea has a floor for them, 50 below the water (§18.4, Sinking and Falling Wrecks).
  * **Hovercraft:** Skim seamlessly across water and land, bypassing naval choke points to flank shorelines.

**Status:** Flight, fuel, the Airfield, aircraft patrols, the air factories of all three tiers and their aircraft, and anti-air of all three tiers are built (§18.4, Aircraft, Fuel and Airfield, and T2 and T3 Aircraft). Rearming waits for aircraft with ammunition. The torpedo bomber waits for naval units, and the transport for transporting. Surface ships are built (§18.4, Ships): naval factories of all three tiers, with support factories, and the frigate, destroyer, cruiser, battleship, and battlecruiser. So are submarines, sonar, and torpedoes (§18.4, Under the Sea). So are the escort corvette, anti-torpedo charges, the strategic missile submarine, and the mobile missile launcher (§18.4, Shields That Move and Missiles From Units). Hovercraft and seabed walkers are built (§18.4, Amphibious).

## 11. The Veterancy System

Units are not static; as they destroy hostile forces, they accumulate experience equal to the Materiel value of their targets, progressing through **5 Veterancy Ranks**:

```
[Rank 0: Recruit] ──► [Rank 1] ──► [Rank 2] ──► [Rank 3] ──► [Rank 4] ──► [Rank 5: Elite]
```

* **XP Thresholds:** Rank n requires n times the unit's own materiel cost times a multiplier for its tech level: 2 at T1, 1.5 at T2, 1.25 at T3, and 2 for field rigs and experimentals. The commander has its own steps, at 1,000, 2,250, 4,000, 6,500, and 10,000 materiel.
* **Veterancy Rewards:**
  * **Max Health Increase:** Each rank adds 10% of base hit points, +50% at rank 5.
  * **Instant Partial Healing:** Reaching a new rank heals 10% of base health at once; the commander heals 10% more at each rank, up to 50% at rank 5.
  * **Passive Health Regeneration:** A flat regeneration a second for each rank: 1 at T1, 3 at T2, 6 at T3 and for commanders, 9 for field rigs, and 25 for experimentals. Ships below experimental take the next row up, so a battleship regenerates 9 a second a rank.
* **Commander & Experimental Dominance:** A Rank 5 commander, Field Rig, or Experimental unit regenerates hundreds of HP per second, turning surviving veteran units into nearly unkillable juggernauts.

## 12. Exotic Weaponry, EMP & Status Effects

* **EMP & Stun Weapons:** EMP missiles and specialized artillery do not deal lethal damage. Instead, they discharge electromagnetic pulses that freeze unit firing mechanisms, disable radar, and shut down shield domes for fixed durations.
* **Tractor Beams:** A tractor beam picks up an enemy land unit, holds it immobilized in mid-air, and destroys it.
* **Death Storms:** Upon destruction, a unit with an unstable reactor leaves a storm that roams the battlefield for 60 seconds, damaging any units in its path.

**Status:** Stuns, tractor beams, and death storms are built as mechanics any unit's data can use (§18.4, Exotic Weaponry). The units that carry them wait for a second faction and experimentals (§14).

## 13. Unit Formations, Movement & Macro Automation

### 13.1 Cohesive Formations
* When a diverse fleet (slow artillery + medium tanks + fast scouts) is given a formation move command, the engine **locks the entire army's speed to the slowest unit**.
* Prevents fast scouts from sprinting ahead and dying piecemeal before heavy fire support arrives.
* Giving a non-formation move order breaks the lock, letting fast units sprint at maximum speed.

**Status:** Built (§18.4). A formation move keeps the group's shape all the way to its destination, at the slowest unit's pace. A plain group move ends in the same orderly grid, but each unit travels to it on its own, at its own speed.

### 13.2 Automated Patrol Loops
Issuing a `Patrol` order across waypoints turns units into automated patrol guardians. Each unit's loop starts where it stands, so it keeps coming back there, or, given with Shift, where its queued orders end: it carries those out first, then patrols from there. Patrol is given as in the inspiration: P, then a click patrols there at once, and with Shift held, patrol stays armed and each further click adds a point to the loop. A patrol given with Shift to a unit already patrolling adds its points to the end of the loop. Before, points were placed first and the patrol started on a right-click or a second press of P, and a Shift-patrol to a unit already patrolling replaced its patrol.
* **Combat Units:** Patrol the route, automatically engage enemies within sensory range, pursue within a leash radius, and return to the patrol loop.
* **Engineers:** Automatically repair damaged friendly buildings, assist active construction, and vacuum up wreckage debris within their patrol radius.
* **Aircraft:** Patrol air lanes, dogfight hostile air, and automatically divert to the nearest Airfield when fuel drops below 20%, returning to patrol once refueled.

### 13.3 Advanced Logistics
* **Coordinated Attacks (Time-on-Target):** Orders multiple strike groups from across the map to arrive and strike a target at the exact same synchronized second.
* **Automated Ferry Routes:** Air transports can be assigned continuous ferry loops. Newly constructed ground units walk to the pickup beacon, board transports, fly to the drop zone, and march to the front autonomously.
* **Custom Geometric Build Templates:** Players can save exact base layouts (e.g., optimized factory-pgen-storage rings) and place the blueprint with a single click.

### 14.2 A Second Faction (Later)
A second faction comes once the first is balanced, built only from the mechanics that already exist. It does not take the genre's usual faction identities (one side's shields, another's stealth and regeneration, a third's hover), and every unit passes the reskin test: if it can be described as another game's unit with a different model, its concept changes (its role, movement, weakness, or scale), not only its name.

One to three T4 units, designed from roles the roster needs and from the setting's own logic, each with a clear, equal-cost answer (§15). ## 16. Game Modes & Victory Conditions

1. **Assassination (Standard Competitive Mode):** The match ends when a player’s commander is destroyed. All structures and units belonging to that player immediately self-destruct or go neutral.
2. **Annihilation:** The match ends only when all enemy mobile units, factories, and combat structures are completely eliminated. Losing a commander does not end the match.
3. **Supremacy:** A player is eliminated if they lose both their commander and all Field Rigs or all factories.
4. **Shared Armies & Reclaim Permissions:** Teammates can dynamically transfer units, economic income percentages, or full army control to allies.

**With teams (specified 2026-11-22, §17.2):** under Assassination, a player is out when their commander is destroyed, and everything they own self-destructs; it does not pass to an ally. Their team plays on, and loses when all its players are out. If the last players of both teams go out on the same tick, the match is a draw, as two commanders destroyed together are today.

## 17. Opponents & Multiplayer

### 17.1 Opponents

* **First Playable:** One human against a scripted AI. The AI issues commands through the same `SimCommand` interface as a player and sees only what its team's intel allows.
* **Difficulty:** The player's opponent is Easy unless they ask for Normal (`--ai normal`, or `?ai=normal` in the browser). Easy keeps a small economy and army, at most 3 engineers of any tier (Normal keeps 3 T2 and 2 T3 engineers on top of its others, and Easy none, so its replacements come at its factories' tier), 2 land factories, 15 land combat units, 4 aircraft, 3 ships, and 1 point defense, counting what its factories are already making, decides every 3 seconds instead of every second, and plans as if it had half its income, so it climbs the tech tree late. It defends from the start but attacks only after 12 minutes, in waves of 6 growing to 12, and never fires missiles or discharge or sends its commander. Planning on half its income alone made it stronger, not weaker: it stopped spending on its economy and tech and put everything into early units, and beat Normal in all six test matches. With the caps, Normal fields at least half as many units again by 20 minutes. The land cap was 16 until 2026-11-27, when point defenses and shield generators were priced for their reach and a dearer shield generator left Normal 36 units to Easy's 25 on one test map (seed 1; Normal fielded 45 to 68 on seeds 2 to 6). At 15, Normal fields 41 to Easy's 23 there. AI-vs-AI matches, and so the soak, always play Normal.
* **Automated Matches:** AI-vs-AI matches run headlessly, serving as full-match regression tests and as the first tool for balance tuning.
* **Multiplayer (Later):** Lockstep over the network, exchanging only commands and comparing checksums to detect desyncs. Matchmaking, lobbies, and transports are not yet specified.
* **Kinds of AI (specified 2026-11-22):** an AI has a difficulty, how well it plays, and a strategy, what it plays, set apart for each AI in a match. Difficulties stay Easy and Normal, with Hard later. Strategies:

| Strategy | Plays |
| :--- | :--- |
| Balanced | Today's Normal: a bit of everything, by what it sees |
| Rush | T1 in numbers, and an early attack |
| Tech | Economy first, then T2 and T3 quickly |
| Air | Power, then bombers and fighters |
| Naval | Ships, on maps with water |
| Turtle | Defends and never sets out (today's Turtle difficulty, which becomes a strategy) |

  "Random" picks one from the match's seed, so a match replays the same. A strategy is data, an entry in `data/ai_strategies.toml` (its unit mix, its tech timings, when it first attacks), not code of its own, so new ones are written and tuned without changing the AI. The balance rule is `[strategy]`'s: each strategy wins inside its timing window and loses outside it, and none wins more than 55% over every opponent and map. A single matchup may be far from even, as Rush catching Tech mid-build should be, so long as it is paid back elsewhere. Naval is judged only on maps with water. A soak plays every strategy against every other to check it. AIs on a team share what they see, come to an ally under attack, and time their attacks together. On a map without a land route (§17.3, Scattered Isles) the AI reaches the enemy by sea and air, and later by transport.

  **Status (2026-11-22):** strategies are built and chosen with `--strategy` or `?strategy=` (or `--ai turtle`, Normal playing Turtle); the soak still plays Balanced against Balanced, and the strategy against strategy soak, which the balance rule needs, is next. A strategy scales its difficulty's numbers: what its land factories build at each tier in turn; the incomes at which it upgrades factories of every kind and extractors, and builds air and naval factories; how many land and air factories it keeps; the size of its waves; its point defenses; when it may first set out and first upgrade; whether a factory may upgrade with no other making units; and which factories, air or naval, it builds before more deposits. The first values came from a match each against Balanced (`tests/ai_strategy_test.rs` checks every strategy plays so): Rush sets out at 4 minutes against 10, with three times the land units at 15 minutes; Tech upgrades its first land factory a minute sooner and has T3 by 20; Air builds its first air factory at 2 minutes against 5, and has four times the aircraft at 15; Naval has its first naval factory at 5 minutes on Twin Passes against 16; and Turtle never sets out. With Balanced's settings the AI plays exactly as before, which the golden AI match checks.

### 17.2 Teams (specified 2026-11-22)

* **Players and teams:** up to 8 players on 2 teams; no free-for-all. A player has their own commander, economy, factories, and color, and is a human or an AI with its own difficulty and strategy. A team is allies: they share sight and radar, never fire at each other, and win or lose together. They do not share an economy, as in the inspiration; gifting units and resources (§16.4) comes later. Elimination is §16's.
* **Matches:** one human with any mix of AI allies and enemies, such as 2v2 or 4v4, or AI against AI for the soak. Networked play stays as above, later.
* **Status (2026-11-22):** built for 2v2 on Saddleback, and on Stillwater and Scattered Isles since 2026-11-24: `--teams 2v2` or `?teams=2v2` (the page's Players menu) puts the person on Blue with an AI ally, against two AIs, at the map's team starts; a map without starts for the count says so. Each player owns their units (`UnitStats::owner`), economy, commander, and start; everything that spends, earns, reclaims, pays upkeep, or gives adjacency does so by its owner; a player whose commander dies is out with everything they own, and a team once all its players are. Since 2026-11-24 also 3v3 on Saddleback and 4v4 on Scattered Isles, on the page as well, whose Players menu offers only the matches the map chosen has starts for. In a team match the score panel at the top right lists each team, with its players' totals, and under it each player: their mass income and their army's worth, with "Out" beside the name of a player who is out (their units in sight too while watching). Income and base are shown for the viewer's own team and hidden for the enemy's, and a watcher seeing everything sees all; clicking a player whose base the viewer knows, the person's own and their allies', moves the camera there. A 1v1 shows a line for each side, as before. A watcher's economy panel adds up each team's players. An AI plays a player: its own forces and economy, home at its own start, against the nearest enemy start and the nearest enemy commander still in the match, and it defends its allies' bases as well as its own. The person cannot select or order an ally's units, which are drawn and dotted on the minimap in a lighter shade of the team's color, cyan beside Blue and orange beside Red. The soak plays 60 2v2s on Saddleback with each push, played and replayed, and 60 3v3s on it and 20 4v4s on Scattered Isles. Not yet: AIs timing their attacks together, support factories needing the player's own headquarters rather than the team's, and gifting units and resources.
* **Engine:** Player is split from Team: what a side owns now (an economy, a commander, the AI's state, colors, and everything held two to a match) becomes one per player, and sight one per team. Allies' units are told apart by color on the units and the minimap. The golden checksums and the soak cover team matches, and a 4v4 on a 20 km map keeps to the soak's tick-time bound.

### 17.3 Maps (specified 2026-11-22; built 2026-11-24)

* **Types and settings:** a map is a type and a few settings, from which its terrain is generated with whole-number math, the same on every machine. The types, beside Highland's open hills and Twin Passes' divided sea:

| Type | Shape | What it rewards |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Passes | A mountain range across the middle, impassable to land units, with valleys through it | Holding chokepoints, artillery behind ridges, shields over a valley, raids through the pass not watched |
| Central Lake | A lake in the middle, with land around it | Mixed play: land armies go the long way round, ships and hovercraft cut across |
| Islands | Each team on its own island, with no land route between them | Navy and air; land armies defend home and cross by hovercraft, the sea floor, and later transports |

The same settings and seed always make the same map.
* **Named maps and the generator:** both. Named maps are a curated list, each a type with its settings fixed, its size among them, and kept fair by the soak. Each has starts for its most players, and lists which starts are used for each count of players, so that a 1v1 on a four-player map uses two starts that face each other, not neighbors; unused starts stay empty, their deposits there for the taking. Named maps are tested for 1v1 and 2v2, 3v3 where they hold 6, and 4v4 where they hold 8; uneven teams are allowed but not promised fair. At match setup, "Generate a map" takes a type and the settings instead.

A unit on the edge is now beside the footprint, whichever edge, and one as near one side of it as another goes out toward the middle of the map. It showed first on a generated Central Lake, at tick 2,391 of the mirror test; the bundled maps' openings had not happened to put an engineer there.
* **A fault in team matches, found by Stillwater's first soak (2026-11-24):** its 1v1s came out even, 64 to 55 with a draw, but its 2v2s went 49 to 11 for Blue, and Saddleback's had been leaning the same way, 38 to 22. In a 2v2 each player's opposite number is their image turned about the map's center, and the teams did not play as mirror images: each unit takes a turn at work done every so many ticks (looking for a target, refreshing its sight, checking whether the way straight to its goal is clear), and the turn was counted by team, so a player's units took turns one off their opposite number's, and the teams parted within 700 ticks. Turns are now counted by player, which leaves a 1v1 exactly as it was. A test holds the teams of a 2v2 to mirrored openings on Saddleback, Stillwater, and Scattered Isles; the soak after this change shows whether the lean has gone.
* **The AI starved of energy, found looking into why island matches waited for the late push (2026-11-24):** on Scattered Isles both AIs made far more mass than they spent, their mass stores full and running over from about tick 6,000, and by tick 20,000 each had 8 ships and 2 aircraft. Their naval yards and air factories started nothing while stored energy was under a quarter, and a half, of what the store held, and the AI built two generators at a time however fast its mass income grew, so energy income only kept level with what was spent and the store sat near empty. Now a factory of energy-hungry units waits only while energy is running short (less coming in than going out, and the store low), and while energy lags mass income the AI has one more generator under way at once for each 100 of mass income a second, past the first two. A full-size wave of strike aircraft now goes for the enemy's base, or its commander where seen, with half the fighters as escort; smaller waves still go for the nearest structure in sight, which on islands was only ever an outlying extractor. In three matches on Scattered Isles each side then had 11 to 14 ships and 35 to 41 aircraft by tick 10,000, and 75 to 116 aircraft by tick 20,000; fleets fought each other, and the three went Red, Red, Blue. They still ended between ticks 21,000 and 25,600, after the late push at 20,000: full air waves at a base were shot down by its anti-air, and the ordinary waves do not yet break a defended home island. What would: fleets placed to shell a base from the sea, strategic bombers sent at the commander, and missiles. Aircraft come sooner and in greater numbers on every map, since the same gates held them back there.
* **Faults that favored a side, found looking into Twin Passes (2026-11-24):** Twin Passes' 1v1s had gone 48 to 70 for Red in the soak (p 0.053), the most lopsided of its maps; they had been 57 to 61 for weeks, and moved with changes that touched only how play went there, on the same 120 seeds each time. Its openings mirrored, but matches on it, and on the other maps from files, parted soon after. Each of these favored one side; all are fixed, and a test plays a match on each of Highland, Twin Passes, Stillwater (2v2), and Saddleback (3v3) as mirror images to tick 9,000, each of which parted before its fix. (1) A rock's size, and so the mass it holds, was drawn for each piece of a map file's scenery, so a rock and its image differed and one side had more to reclaim near its base, a different amount every seed; a rock and its images are now one size, as on the skirmish map. (2) Flow fields were built eight a tick in the order they were asked for, which put one player's behind another's whenever more were asked for, so a unit could start on its way a tick after its mirror image; each player now has the budget of eight to themself. (3) The middle of a group, which a transport heads for to pick it up and a formation is laid out from, was a mean rounded toward zero, one way for one side's positions and the other way for its mirror's, and a formation's places were rounded toward negative infinity; both now round alike for mirror images. (4) Aircraft took an airfield's pads south-west first, for both sides; they now take first the one nearest the map's middle. (5) A shot leading an aircraft in a turn, and the point where a shot passes closest to its target, were rounded toward negative infinity, so a shot could hit on one side where its image missed; both round toward zero, as do the points where shots strike. (6) A unit exactly on the map's middle row or column was in the same cell as its mirror image, not its image's cell. (7) A land wave went for the first enemy structure in the list, the oldest, which in a team match was one enemy player's for one team and the other's for the other; it now goes for the one nearest home. With these, many AI matches play as mirror images to the end and are drawn, both commanders falling on the same tick: played to tick 16,000 along the way, most matches on every map never parted, and three Twin Passes seeds ended drawn. Some still part after tick 10,000 (Twin Passes seed 3, Highland seed 3, and seed 2 of a Saddleback 2v2), for faults not yet found. The soak's leans should shrink, and its draws grow.
* **Scattered Isles' first soak, and what it changed in the AI (2026-11-24):** of its 120 1v1s, 16 ran past the 40,000-tick limit, which fails the soak, and the rest went 63 to 41 for Blue; its 2v2s went 37 to 23, before the fault above was fixed. The AIs there made far more than they spent, with some seventy extractors each feeding two land factories and two naval yards, and nothing decided a match until sudden death, when the commanders set out across the seabed toward each other and often stopped in mid-sea, each once the other had gone under, with the enemy's fleet at anchor off its base. Three changes, all only where there is no land route, except the last: a naval yard more for each 40 of mass income past the second's, up to four, and no cap of two on land factories once the headquarters is T2, since each new one makes hovercraft; the late push and sudden death come 6,000 ticks sooner (at 20,000 and 24,000), since the way across takes a commander some eight minutes; and in sudden death, on any map, the fleet goes for the enemy commander while it is under the sea, where only ships can hit it, every ship sent again every 300 ticks as the army is. Replayed inside a test, all 16 seeds that had not finished now do, the eight slowest of them between ticks 25,300 and 26,350, four to each side. Matches there are still decided by the late push, not before it: the AI's ordinary waves of hovercraft, ships, and aircraft do not yet break a defended home island.

### 18.4 Implementation Decisions
These are starting points for tuning, recorded because they interpret or adjust the design above.

* **Scale:** One terrain cell (20 units) is the game's basic grid square. Unit sizes, speeds, and ranges in `data/units.toml` are set in proportion to it, and the skirmish map is 10 km across.
* **Mirror Fairness:** On a mirrored map, a unit and its mirror image heading the opposite way move exactly alike: a direction is scaled by a speed, a distance, or an angle's cosine with rounding toward zero, where the fixed-point type's own product rounds toward negative infinity. Before this, the side heading toward lower coordinates moved a 4,096th of a unit further on every step. It wore away the mirror within a few minutes, always the same way, and the AI-vs-AI soak went 255 to 193 for Blue; afterwards 127 to 120. Tie-breaks and staggered work that went by unit id or by row and column then still favored a side, since one team's ids are lower and one corner's rows come first: in AI-vs-AI soaks the team with the lower ids won 117 to 83, and with the ids swapped lost 89 to 111. Work spread over ticks (looking for targets, refreshing sight and sensors, patrolling builders looking for work) now goes by each unit's place among its own team's units, which a unit and its mirror image share. Where a walkable cell nearest a point, or a flow field's way out of a cell, is tied, the choice is the one nearer the map's center, and of those the one turning anticlockwise about it, rules that turning the map 180° leaves alone. A route also prepares every next sector that ties for the nearest, not only the first in its list. On seeds 1 to 3 the two sides now stay exact mirror images, within a twentieth of a unit, past tick 5,000, where they parted at ticks 3,800 to 4,500 before; `cargo run --release --example mirror_probe` finds where they part, and `mirror_trace` follows a unit and its mirror image up to then. A unit exactly on, or within a few raw bits of, the line between two cells counts as in the cell nearer the map's center, for whether it may stand there and which way its flow field sends it (`Terrain::unit_cell_of`): rounding down put Blue's unit on a line in the cell east of it and Red's mirror image in the cell east of it too, one apart from the mirror, and the two went different ways. "Within a few raw bits", because rounding can leave one side a bit short of the line its mirror is on. A transport sets its cargo down on the nearest open cells to its spot, trying cells equally near in an order turned about the center for Red: in one order for both, a drop on Twin Passes put Red's units two cells off Blue's, turned about the center. On Saddleback (2026-11-22), turned a quarter so that its 1v1 starts faced each other across the middle row, Red won three matches in five, for three reasons, each a tie broken the same way for both sides: the starts stood on the line through the map's middle, where a unit's two nearest cells are equally near the center, so the sides parted on the first tick (starts now stand in the middle of a cell); the AI measured a vent's distance from the corner cell of the plant it would build, which lies to one side (it now measures from the vent, ties going by the vent as Blue's side sees it); and it picked a deposit to raid by index in the map's list, which gave mirrored deposits only where a map listed them in mirrored pairs, as Saddleback, listing them in fours, does not (it now sorts them as Blue's side sees them). The sides now stay mirror images through the opening there, which `tests/mirror_test.rs` checks.
* **Commander Explosion:** §2.1 gives a 25-meter radius, which is barely more than one grid square and could not "vaporize surrounding bases". The slice uses 300 units, as a data value.
* **Friendly Fire:** A shell ignores allies until it reaches its aim point, so that rear ranks can fire through front ranks. Rounds that overshoot can hit allies, and splash damages everyone, which keeps the stray rounds and artillery spread of §7.1.
* **Fog of War:** Sight is a simple radius, not blocked by terrain. Units cannot target what their team cannot see, but for enemy structures it has found, which it remembers (§9, Remembered Structures).
* **Upkeep:** Shields are the only upkeep so far, and are paid before building, so that building takes what is left. A team that cannot pay for a dome loses it (see Shields). Sensor effects from §3.2 arrive with sensors.
* **Orders During Construction:** A factory takes queue, repeat, rally, and upgrade orders while it is under construction. They wait at no cost, and production starts once it is built.
* **Work Beams:** A builder faces what it is working on, joined to it by an animated beam, which covers building, assisting, repairing, and reclaiming. The beam is in the world, not over it: a glowing rod with pulses running along it, toward the work or back when reclaiming, and sparks flying about the work, hidden behind what stands in front of it and left out when zoomed out to icons. A team with nothing to spend shows no beams, so a beam always means progress. This is presentation only, and not part of the checksum.
* **Control Groups:** Ctrl or Command plus a number saves the selection, and with nothing selected clears that group. The number alone selects it. A bar on the left lists the groups in use and what is in them, and clicking one selects it. Dead units drop out.
* **Turning:** Each mobile unit has a turn rate, in degrees per second, and may have a turn radius, in units. A unit only moves forward along its facing. With a turn radius it turns like a car: only while moving, in an arc no tighter than its radius, slowing to half speed in a tight turn but never stopping, so a U-turn swings out to the side. A goal inside the circle it would turn in cannot be reached by driving on, so it pivots slowly on the spot instead, as a tracked vehicle would. Without a turn radius it turns on the spot, as a walker does. The commander's legs and the engineer's hull follow the facing, while the body and arm above turn freely to face their work. Values: commander 180 degrees a second on the spot, engineer 240 with a 10-unit radius, light bot 360 on the spot, light artillery 120 with 30, heavy tank 90 with 40. Stress test units turn instantly. Planned: naval units turn slowly, and air units cannot stop or turn on the spot, so they must fly in arcs and circle; their turn rate matters most.
* **Turrets:** Each weapon has a turn rate, in degrees per second, and swivels towards its target by at most that much each tick. It only fires when it points within about 6 degrees of the target. Without a target it turns back over the hull. The commander's gun arm, the bot's upper body, the tank's turret, the artillery's barrel, and the point defense's gun are drawn from it. Rates: commander 180, light bot 300, light artillery 90, heavy tank 60, point defense 120. Stress test units fire in any direction at once.
* **Unit Paint:** Each part of a unit's shape is painted one of four ways: team color, armor, dark gunmetal, or a glow. Armor is grey, taking on a little of the unit's own tint, which shows what it is, and a little of the team color. Gunmetal is the same for everyone, for guns, tracks, legs, and masts. Glowing parts, such as engines, emitters, and the commander's visor, take the unit's tint at full brightness and are never shaded, and a structure's parts glow only once it is finished. Every unit shows its team color somewhere that can be seen from above. Colors are chosen as they look on screen and lit in linear light, with light from the sky above, the ground below, and the sun, a soft shine, and a faint light along edges so that outlines show against the ground. A unit shrunk to a tactical dot is all team color.
* **Unit Models:** Every unit and structure is an original low-poly model, built in code from blocks, beveled blocks, prisms, drums, and arches, and shaped so that what it is can be read from above. Ships have a dark band at the waterline and a raised deck at the bow, and each class has its own outline from above and its own layout: the frigate is short, with a square stern carrying a team-colored helipad and a single gun forward; the destroyer long and lean, six and a half times as long as it is wide, with a long raked bow, a gun at each end, a raked funnel, and torpedo tubes; the cruiser widest amidships, where sponsons carry its two team-colored radar domes out past its sides, so that from above it is a cross, with missile cells fore and aft and a radar mast; the battleship broad, barely three times as long as wide, with bulged sides, a blunt round bow, and three twin turrets clear of a stepped tower, with two anti-air guns each side of it and two missile defense mounts on its top, two forward, the second on a barbette over the first, and one aft, each turning to its own gun's aim and resting toward the bow or the stern, and each gun firing from its own turret; the battlecruiser long and narrow, with a laser turret on its forecastle and one on its quarterdeck, each a drum under a long ribbed emitter that turns to its own aim, a great raked tower under twin funnels, a missile defense mount behind its bridge, and a torpedo sponson out from each side amidships; the torpedo boat a short, wide wedge with a square stern, torpedo tubes along its sides, and a team-colored chevron on its foredeck; and the escort corvette a trimaran, a slim hull between two outriggers joined by a team-colored cross deck under its tall shield emitter. Submarines are rounded, with a tower amidships, and the missile submarine has two long rows of glowing hatches. Factories, generators, and extractors look different at each tier, with more halls, stacks, gantries, towers, and lights as they go up: a T2 bay has higher buttressed walls and two cranes, a T3 bay sloped armor, a double gantry with a cab, and a control tower, and the air and naval factories bigger hangars, more cranes, and towers. Aircraft have bodies that taper to a point, wings thick at the root and thin at the tips, small slanted canopies, and glowing engine nozzles. From above, an aircraft's wing tells its role: fighters' wings sweep back 40 degrees or more (the interceptor 48, the air superiority fighter 58), bombers' 15 or less (the bomber 9, the torpedo bomber 6, the strategic bomber 8), and the fighter-bomber's 28, between the two. The strategic bomber is the largest aircraft with wings, 44 long and as wide, larger than the heavy transport: a long body under a long, straight, team-colored wing with four engines slung under it, and a tailplane with twin fins, where the bomber's body is team-colored and its wing armor (until 2026-11-20 it was a swept flying wing). Gunships have streamlined bodies with a tail boom and four slim rotor blades, the heavy gunship two rotors, and bots carry a gun in one arm. The commander has a team-colored chest with a glowing core, a backpack with glowing vents, shoulder pads, a gun on its weapons bay, and a helmet with a glowing visor, on jointed legs with feet. Engineers have tracks and a jointed arm on a team-colored turntable, tanks have fenders, engine grilles, and turrets with mantlets and muzzle brakes, artillery braces on spades, and turrets stand on stepped plinths with a team-colored ring. Support factories look like the tier they build. Every weapon and defense a unit has is a part of its model: a ship's anti-air guns, its missile defense, and its torpedo tubes are each there to be seen, and nothing is modeled that the unit does not have. The one exception is a ship's anti-torpedo charges, which leave from its hull under the water, and so are not modeled on any ship. A weapon that several units carry is the same model on each, at the same size: the tactical missile defense is one mount, a white dome over a team-colored band on a column, on the land structure and on the cruiser, the battleship, and the battlecruiser alike, each ship drawn with as many as it carries, and the frigate's, the destroyer's, and the battleship's anti-air guns are the T1 Anti-Air Turret's gun, though each does its own damage.
* **Unit Animation:** Units move as well as travel. Walkers' legs swing through strides as far as they walk, and come back straight when they stop. Rotors and radar dishes turn. Guns kick back with a muzzle flash as they fire, for a moment after each shot. Structures being built stand in scaffolding as tall as they will be, until they are finished, and factories' and builders' lights shine while they work and dim while idle. All of it follows the game's own clock, so that it stops while the game is paused, and none of it is ever read by the simulation.
* **Sticky Targets:** A unit on an attack-move, or on a patrol, keeps the target it picks until the target is destroyed, as if told to attack it, and chases it out of range and out of its own sight, then goes on with its move. While the target is near, within 400 of its range, and the way to it open, the unit walks straight at it. Further off, or past a wall, it is given an attack order on the target, which finds a way there, and its move waits at the front of its queue. A target it cannot reach is passed over for 30 seconds. A unit with a bomb bay keeps its target this way on any order: each bombing pass carries a bomber past its target, out of range and out of sight under fog, and dropping the target then sent the next bomb at whatever was nearest, so that against moving tanks it switched targets five times before destroying one. An explicit attack order already chased its target until it was destroyed. Only a cloak, a new order, or a change that puts the target beyond the unit's weapon, or too close for artillery, breaks a chase off. Builders on an attack-move do not chase, since their walks are among their tasks. Giving every chaser in a 5,000-unit battle a route of its own made the slowest ticks four times as slow, so short chases are walked straight.
* **Effects:** Every explosion shows how far its damage reaches: shells, bombs, missiles, and Discharges burst into a fireball that swells to the width of the blast that does their damage, and no wider (a test checks every weapon and every unit that blows up as it dies), and units that blow up as they die, such as the command rig and the generators, burst as wide as their blast, which the snapshot gives the renderer (until 2026-11-18 their fireball was only as big as the unit, and every fireball swelled a quarter past its blast). Units explode as they die, with a fireball and smoke that rises and drifts away, larger for larger units. Smoke is see-through, thinner at its edges and as it ages, and no two puffs are quite alike in shape, size, or shade. Missiles leave a trail of smoke that spreads and fades behind them, with a flame at the tail, and point the way they fly, climbing from the launcher and diving onto the target included (until 2026-11-24 a missile was drawn level whatever its climb, so that one going up looked to fly sideways), and their blasts are bigger, a strategic missile's far bigger. Since 2026-11-24 a blast looks like what made it: big fireballs billow in four lobes of orange fire about a white-hot core, together as wide as the blast (until then one pale ball, a flat yellow disc from above); a round with no blast is a spark and a puff of dust; artillery and bombs throw up columns of earth, T3 artillery and bombs a ring of dust as well; a battleship's round, which looks like T2 artillery's in flight, lands with less of a bang, no earth thrown up; flak bursts into black smoke in the air; a shell on the water throws up spray rather than earth, and a torpedo a tall column of water; a tactical missile sends a ring of dust out over the ground; and a strategic missile's blast is a mushroom cloud: a white flash, a shock ring running out over the ground, a skirt of dust round its foot, and a fireball rising on a column of dust into a broad cap that billows out as it climbs, glowing orange, then greying, over some ten seconds, its smoke staying thick until near the end, where other smoke thins all its life. The renderer works out which weapon fired a shell from the unit that fired it, matching the shell's blast to one of its weapons; a shell from a gun out of sight is guessed from its blast. `examples/effect_sheet.rs` draws every weapon's shot in flight and its blast at a few moments after into one picture. Ships on the surface leave foam behind them that spreads and fades on the water. What lands on the ground leaves a crater, a dark bowl ringed with thrown-up earth, and units that die on the ground scorch it black, so that a battlefield shows where the fighting was. The marks are only drawn: the ground's shape never changes, so they make no difference to play. They fade away over a few minutes, and nothing is marked under water. Effects are drawn from what the player can see and never touch the game, and they stop while it is paused.
* **Terrain and Water:** Ground is grass, darker in the lows and drier on the heights, in soft patches with bare dirt here and there, rock where it is steep, in layers of lighter and darker stone with cracks between, and sand along the shore, with faint contour lines on gentle ground for scale. Hills are lit smoothly, and hollows, gullies, and the feet of slopes are darker while crests catch a little more light, so that the shape of the land reads from far away. Up close, small bumps break up the light, and the grass is speckled with lighter and darker blades and small stones, all of which fade away as the camera rises so that nothing shimmers at a distance. Water is a see-through surface: clear and pale in the shallows, dark and deep blue further out, with the ground below darkening with depth, the sky showing in it at a glancing angle, a gentle drifting shimmer, and foam where it laps the shore. Ships float in it, and submarines show dimly through it.
* **Shadows:** Units, structures, aircraft, trees, and rocks cast shadows with their own outlines, away from the sun, onto the ground or the water. An aircraft's shadow is lighter the higher it flies, and overlapping shadows are no darker than one. Shadows fade with their units into tactical dots, and anything under water casts none. Edges are smoothed by drawing each pixel from several samples.
* **Shield Look:** A dome is drawn as a bubble over what it covers: almost clear where it is seen face on, with faint hexagons on its surface and a thin bright rim, so that the base inside stays easy to read. Only its near side is drawn, its hexagons fade before they are small enough to shimmer, and it fades out as the camera comes close, so that it never hides the view from inside. It grows fainter as the shield wears down.
* **Playtest Sandbox:** `--map playtest` (`?map=playtest`): a 10 km map, land to the west and sea to the east. Blue has 5 of every unit but the commander, so it cannot lose, 100,000 materiel and 5,000,000 energy, enough for a strategic missile launcher and several nuclear missiles, and two T3 generators with energy storage behind its army, far more energy than its shields draw, so that none goes down: land units in rows on its side, aircraft parked to the south, ships and submarines out at sea. Red has an anti-air range along the south edge of the land, a row of four T1 anti-air turrets, then four flak cannons, then four SAM launchers, west to east, about 2 km apart, with two cruisers offshore near its main base, for trying each aircraft against each tech level of anti-air; and two bases on the coast, each with a T2 land factory, extractors and generators, point defense facing the land and the sea, anti-air, a shield, a radar, a tactical missile defense, and torpedo launchers, and the main one also an air factory, a naval factory, an artillery installation, and its commander. It is played against the Turtle AI unless another is asked for (`--ai`): the full AI, with three times the point defenses, that meets anything coming near home but never sends its army, fleet, or bombers out, and fires no missiles and makes no drops.
* **Unit Database:** `?units` in the browser, linked from the landing page, lists every unit and structure with all its numbers: cost, build time, health, movement, sight and sensors, economy, shields and defenses, what it builds, and each weapon's damage, rate, damage a second, range, blast, shot speed, and turret. It is grouped by kind (land, air, naval, structures) and tech level, with each unit's portrait, and filters by kind, tech level and name. Any units picked on it can be compared side by side (`?units#compare=t2_cruiser,t2_destroyer`, so a comparison can be linked to): one column a unit, row by row, with the cheapest, toughest and hardest-hitting marked and an option to show only what differs, read from the cards themselves, so it shows just what they do. The game writes the page itself, from the very blueprints it plays with (`data/units.toml`), so there is no second copy of any number to fall out of step.
* **Playtest Scenarios:** `?scenarios` in the browser, linked from the landing page, lists the fights the balance harness measures, from `data/scenarios.toml`, to play by hand with fog of war on or off (`?scenario=<id>&fog=on|off`, or `--scenario <id>` on the desktop): T3 artillery sieging a shielded T3 position, defending set up against massed T1, screened by Heavy Walkers, and rushed by raiders; Medium Tanks against massed Light Artillery; a raid; the T1 line mirror; the T2 skirmisher against the line; and bombers against a line with and without anti-air. Each is on flat open ground the duel ground's size. The player has Blue's units only, with no commander, factories, or income; Red's units attack-move at the player from the start or hold their ground, and structures hold; both sides have the energy for shields and artillery. A bar at the top shows each side's share of its materiel left, weighted by health, and the harness's score, the difference; with fog of war on, Red's is hidden. No commander means no match end: reloading the page plays it again. The game builds the scenarios and writes the page from the same file, with each side's units and their materiel, reach, least range, setup, sight, and speed from the unit data.
* **Firing on Radar:** A unit fires at any enemy its team sees or has on radar, deep scan, or, under the water, sonar, within its weapons' reach: it does not need to see it, and it knows what it is, so each weapon picks what it can hit. A cruiser launches at every land target in reach and shoots at every aircraft, by radar as by sight. Stealth keeps a unit off radar unless deep scan strips it, a cloak hides it until an enemy comes close, and a submerged submarine shows only on sonar, as before; decoys are not fired at. Before, only bombers fired at what was on radar, and everything else at what it saw.
* **Orders on Radar:** A right-click on a radar contact, with nothing in sight there, attacks the unit it is. A decoy looks the same, and an attack on it sends the units there, to find nothing. Over an enemy, in sight or on radar, the pointer shows a red target if a right-click attacks it (since 2026-11-26; a crosshair before), and a no-entry sign if nothing selected can hit it, which tells a careful player what a contact is: air, land, a ship, or a submarine under the water.
* **Going Round Friends:** A unit that runs into a friend standing in its way, or going much slower the same way, slides sideways round it, away from the side it is on, instead of shoving it ahead. A builder standing still at work does not count as one of the crowd round a goal, whom a unit bumping into stops beside. And a structure placed on top of units moves them off its footprint, to the nearest open ground beside it. Together these freed engineers coming out of a factory onto a commander building beside it, who used to stay stuck against it, short of the rally point.
* **Factories Assisting Factories:** Right-clicking a factory with other factories selected has them assist it: whenever one has nothing of its own to build, it takes the next item the assisted factory has not started, and can build itself, onto its own queue, and builds it; on repeat, the item goes back to the end of the assisted queue too. What it makes goes to its own rally point, or the assisted factory's. Stop, clearing its queue, or the other factory's loss ends it.
* **Clicking Far-Off Enemies:** A right-click within 22 pixels of an enemy, twice a far-off unit's symbol, is an order at it: attack, capture, or Discharge. Selecting keeps to 12.
* **Launch and the Red Target (2026-11-26):** Everything that fires missiles only on order, the Tactical and Strategic Missile Launchers and the Strategic Missile Submarine, has a Launch button, on L, that shows the missiles stowed out of how many fit, as 2/5 (summed over the selection); the selection panel says the same of one silo. L then a left-click fires the launchers that reach at the enemy there, in sight or on radar, or else at the ground (an aircraft, which missiles cannot hit, is passed over for the ground beneath it); Shift keeps it armed for another. L is the commander's Discharge too, and with a launcher selected it launches. A right-click on an enemy launches as well, as it attacks. A launcher structure that cannot reach the enemy is left out of a right-click's or Launch's order, instead of waiting on a target it will never fire at. Wherever a click would attack or launch, the pointer is a red target (four corner brackets round the spot, since the Interface Look entry below): over an enemy when a right-click there would attack or launch (worked out from the orders the click gives, and the same over a decoy as over what it looks like), and anywhere on the map while Launch or attack ground is armed. Over an enemy that nothing selected can reach or hit, it is the not-allowed pointer, as before; a right-click that captures leaves the usual pointer.
* **Strategic Interceptors (2026-11-26):** A Strategic Missile Defense no longer destroys a nuclear missile the instant it comes in range: it fires an interceptor, drawn as a large pale-blue missile with a thick trail, that flies up to meet the missile further along its path, at most 3 seconds later, and less if the missile would strike sooner. Where they meet is worked out at launch from the missile's path, which is fixed, so it never misses: on the tick it arrives the missile is destroyed, with a flash where they meet, even if the defense has been destroyed meanwhile, and no other defense fires at a missile already chased. A missile that would strike on the very tick it comes in range is destroyed at once, as before. A nuclear missile shot down bursts in the air, a fireball and smoke with no mushroom cloud, no mark on the ground, and no strike heard across the map (until now it was drawn and heard as if it had struck, where it was).
* **Walking Straight:** A unit walks straight at its goal whenever the way there is clear, a strip as wide as the unit along the line, which it looks at when given the order, every 1.6 seconds while it is not, and every 6.4 seconds while it is, in case something has been built in the way. Otherwise it follows its route, round walls and through gaps, until the way is clear. Near the goal, within 100 or three of its turning circles, it keeps straight only while it faces the goal, within about 18 degrees; otherwise its route brings it in, since a unit that turns in arcs, aiming at the very point from beside it, circled it for good. Following a unit, it keeps to its route. Before, every move followed the route's eight directions, through the middle of each sector's edge: a move at 27 degrees strayed 1,400 off the line and went 8% further, and even a diagonal strayed 470.
* **New Orders on the Move:** A unit given a new move keeps going as it was until its new route's way has been worked out, the next tick. It used to stop for that tick, so that a unit sent the same way again and again, as players do with repeated clicks, lurched at half speed each time, and a unit firing at one moving alongside it missed every shell it had aimed by that speed.
* **Unreachable Orders:** An order that cannot be reached is cancelled as soon as a unit's up-to-date flow field shows no way from its cell to the goal. That covers walls and structures today, and impassable mountains, water for land units, and land for ships once those exist, since each is only more cells a unit cannot cross. Builders drop the task, patrols end, queued units move on, and the player is told. A goal that can be reached by a long way round is never cancelled.
* **Moving Orders:** While Shift is held, each order that can move has an icon, and dragging it puts the order somewhere else, for every unit sharing it. Moves and rally points can go anywhere. Builds follow the usual placement rules, so an extractor still snaps to a free deposit, and a build dropped where it cannot go stays where it was. A site that has started, a patrol, an attack, and a follow order cannot be moved.
* **Selection Rates:** The command panel shows what a selected structure or commander makes, including adjacency bonuses, and what a factory is really spending, including help and stalls.
* **Queued Orders:** Shift queues an order after what the selection is already doing, in any mix of moves, attack-moves, builds, assists, repairs, and reclaims. A group shares one route per queued order, and forms up at each stop. A builder finishes a walk it is on before starting what was queued, even if that job is already within reach. A queued attack waits its turn and then goes after its target wherever it has got to, firing only at it, as an attack given straight away does, whether the target is in sight or a contact on radar; a target destroyed before then is skipped, and what was queued after it goes ahead (until 2026-11-20 a queued attack was an attack-move to where the target was when ordered, and one on a radar contact was sent unqueued, replacing what was queued before it), and follow orders and patrols have no end to wait for: following is not queued, and queuing an order ends a patrol. A patrol can itself be queued, as the last thing a unit does, starting from where the queue ends (§13.2).
* **Quick Select Bar:** A bar on the right selects the commander or factories from anywhere, without moving the view. Factories are grouped by kind, then tier, with counts. A tier selects the factory closest to the center of the view, a double-click selects all of them, and Shift adds to the selection. Factories still being built count, as they take orders. A finished factory with nothing queued and no factory to help is idle: its kind's square carries a red hatched tag counting the idle ones, and clicking the tag selects just those, while the rest of the square opens the kind as before; on the map, the factory has a red hatched bar and IDLE under it, drawn over the icons around it, at any zoom. A missile launcher with nothing queued is not idle, as it is ready with what it has (added 2026-11-20).
* **Camera Orbit:** Holding Space and moving the mouse turns the camera round the point on the ground in the middle of the view, which stays there: moving it left and right turns it, up and down tilts it, from 20 degrees above the horizon to almost straight down. Panning follows the screen, whichever way it faces, and the minimap outlines the view as it lies. Letting go puts the camera back over the same point, facing north at the usual tilt for its height. A quick tap that does not move the mouse still jumps to the commander, or pauses a watched match. Added 2026-11-19; before, the camera always faced north.
* **Sound:** Generated in code, and never read by the simulation. Every sound is kept soft enough to hear all match long: nothing sharp, nothing that switches on or off abruptly. Picking units by any means gives a short, soft blip that rises a little, including picking the same units again. It is in stereo: each sound comes from where its source is across the screen, never wholly from one side. Zoomed in, the battlefield is heard as it is seen (2026-11-24): the sea, a soft rush under two slow swells, wherever water is in view; the wash of ships under way; a low, soft rumble of land units and hovercraft moving; and an airy rush of aircraft passing. Each is heard only while it is happening on screen, louder at speed than creeping, and a crowd louder than one but never as many times as loud. A builder at work is heard by its beam: a soft, steady fizz of energy, noise between 1.2 and 3.5 kHz with no tone in it at all, chosen by ear from four candidates (a steady tone, the fizz, a softer, lower rush, and a tone under the fizz). It fades in over a quarter second and out over a third, holds a steady level, and a crowd of builders adds a little of a band below, fuller but hardly louder. A factory making something has the same fizz lower, between 600 Hz and 1.8 kHz. Tones were tried first, all on 2026-11-24, and each failed: soft chimes did not sound like building; a laser of pure tones stacked into a chord (a base, its octave, a fifth, and two octaves up) with a gentle pulse was heard as chiming, the more so while its two lowest voices beat twice a second down to a quarter as loud; and that laser rising in pitch as it started and falling as it stopped, over a noisy sizzle, sounded like a power drill. Before them, builders made a warm hum and factories a press-like swell, too industrial for the game's feel. The fighting is heard too, only in view and zoomed in (in full up to 0.15, gone by 0.45), soft and low-passed, a battle heard rather than a barrage: a gun firing is a soft thump and a puff, lower, longer, and louder for heavier guns; a laser striking a soft, falling "fsss" with no tone in it; a cruise missile leaving a whoosh over a low rumble; a shell or missile striking a low, dropping thump and a dull burst, bigger for a bigger blast; and a unit destroyed, by debris left where it stood (a unit that only goes out of sight leaves none, and is not heard), or by its blast as it dies, a crunching boom that darkens as it dies away, deeper for a bigger unit. For each snapshot, at most four guns, two lasers, two missiles, three strikes, and three units destroyed start, the heaviest and nearest kept and made a little louder for the rest. Every one is struck over 3 to 6 ms, never switched on. Two sounds are meant to be big, and are heard wherever the camera is, in full on screen and softer off it (a third for a commander, two fifths for a strategic missile), from their side of the screen: a commander blowing up as it dies, a deep thump, a roar that darkens over a few seconds, and a rumble under it; and a strategic missile striking, the deepest thump, a roar that swells open over its first second and closes slowly, and a rumble that outlasts it, ten seconds in all. Each dips everything else (to under half for a commander, under a third for a strategic missile) while it lasts, then lets it back over about a second. A strategic missile launched by anyone, seen or not, sounds a low warning: two soft notes, high then low, three times, unlike the high alarm. At most 16 sounds that play once are heard at a time; with all busy, the oldest small one gives way, and a great explosion never does. However many sounds play at once, together they stay about as loud as one at its fullest, each quieter as more join. Everything is quieter than the selection blip, and all of it is silent once the match is over. An alarm, two falling notes struck twice like a chime, and a text alert show when the commander takes damage, then wait 30 seconds, wherever the camera is. Until 2026-11-19 factories buzzed with sawtooth and narrow pulse waves gated five times a second, the pick tick swept up past 2 kHz, and the alarm's notes switched on and off; tests in `src/audio/tests.rs` measure each sound's harshness (its energy above 1 kHz), loudness, how abruptly it rises, and where it sits in the range, and hold them to the softer levels. `cargo run --release --example sound_preview -- <folder>` writes each sound to a WAV file, and `--sound-check` plays each in turn.
* **Unit Order Bar:** Anything that can walk shows Move, Attack-move, Patrol, Assist, and Stop in the command panel. Assist means staying with a friendly unit and firing at what comes into range, without chasing it. Builders also lend their build power if the unit has work that can use it. An armed order is shown on its button, and is cancelled by right-click or Esc.
* **Command Panel Hotkeys:** Every option in the command panel shows its key in bold, which does the same as its button. A build bar opens at the highest tech level the selection can build, every time the selection changes. While a structure is being placed, a ring on the ground around where it would go shows each range it will reach, named and colored by kind: its weapons, and for artillery the range too close to hit, its shield, missile or torpedo defense, radar, sonar, deep scan, and stealth field. Unit orders have a key each. A builder's structures come in families that share a key, the same at every tech level: W factories (land, then air, then naval, and the gateway), E materiel (extractors, fabricators, storage), S energy (generators, the geothermal plant, storage), D point defense, anti-air, and torpedo launchers, A artillery and missile launchers, Z radar, sonar, deep scan, and stealth, C shields and missile defenses, V field workshops, airfield, and walls. A family sits together in the bar, with a divider between families. Its key starts placing the first of the family at the level the bar shows, and pressing it again while placing moves on to the next, round to the first again. A factory's items take the key of their slot in its bar, Q E G T C Z V B, each kind of unit keeping its slot at every level; with a builder and a factory selected together, the factory's bar is shown and takes the keys. The arrow keys pan the view. The key above Tab shows the next level in the bars. N mutes, and - and = set the volume, when they do nothing else. Keys are mostly on the left of the keyboard, because the other hand is on the mouse.
* **Queuing Ahead of an Upgrade:** Once a factory has an upgrade queued, it also takes what the upgrade unlocks. New items go to the end of the queue, so they can only come out after the upgrade has finished. Removing the upgrade removes what depended on it.
* **Removing Queued Orders:** The command panel lists the selection's queue, and right-clicking an item removes one of it: from the selected factory with the most of it, so that five tanks across two factories become four. Left-clicking a factory's item queues one more, at the selected factory holding it with the shortest queue; not another upgrade. Orders are named rather than numbered, because a queue can move on between the click and the command arriving. A site that was already started stays where it is, without a refund, and can be finished by assisting it. A factory loses its last matching item, so that work under way is only lost if it is the only one.
* **Arrival Formations:** A group ordered to a location forms a grid about twice as wide as it is deep, centered on the goal and lined up across the direction of travel. Units follow a shared route, then walk to their own spots, with a time limit so that a blocked spot cannot trap a unit.
* **Production Pausing:** A factory or construction site can be paused with O or the panel button, and resumed the same way. It spends nothing and makes no progress until resumed, keeps its queue and progress, and builders helping it wait beside it. Shields and materiel fabricators will pause the same way when they exist.
* **Discharge:** The commander fires only with 7,500 energy in store, more than it holds by itself, so it needs energy storage, and nothing is spent until the shell lands. There it may spend up to 90% of the store, split between the team's Discharge shells in flight, at 6 energy a point of damage, up to 15,000 damage; of that, it spends only what destroys the toughest enemy mobile unit within 54 of where it lands (health and shield together), and never less than 1,250 (7,500 energy). With no mobile enemy there, or against a lone commander or a structure, it does 1,250. Its blast, 50 across, hurts only enemies: commanders at most 400, and structures and their domes at most 800. Against a mobile unit's dome it does what brings the dome down. It reloads in 3.3 seconds and fires through the commander's gun arm, so it has to aim first. Before this, it was one shot of 12,000 for a flat 3,000 energy, which killed anything mobile far too cheaply. Ordered with L and a click on an enemy, the commander closes to range and fires as soon as it has the energy, and the order ends if the target dies. Values are in the unit data. The AI uses it (AI Defenses and Intel, below).
* **Veterancy:** §11's rules. Every enemy that damaged a unit shares its materiel when it is destroyed, or as much of it as had been built, each by its share of the damage enemies did it, splash included; a hit counts for no more than the unit's whole health. A share earns at most one rank's width at a time, so one kill never jumps two ranks. Nothing is earned for destroying its own side, or by a unit already destroyed. Each rank adds 10% of base health, heals 10% of it at once, and adds flat regeneration by tech level. Before, the one that landed the last blow took it all, rank n came at n times its own cost, and ranks gave 20% health and regeneration of 0.2% of maximum health a second. Stress test units have no materiel, so they earn nothing.
* **Intel:** Each team has radar and deep scan coverage, stamped in turns with sight. An enemy on a team's radar or deep scan that it cannot see shows as a blip: a triangle that says nothing of what it is. The Radar (T1, 60 materiel) covers 2,320 for 10 energy a second, and upgrades in place with U to the T2 Radar (180 materiel and 3,600 energy, 78 seconds on its own build power of 10), which covers 4,000 for 150 energy a second, and that to the Deep Scan Array (T3, 2,400 materiel and 30,000 energy, 120 seconds on the T2 radar's build power of 20, and 100 health), which covers 12,000 with radar and 4,000 with deep scan for 2,000 energy a second. The Stealth Field Generator (T2, 300 materiel, 25 energy a second) hides every friendly unit within 480 from enemy radar, itself included, and puts 8 decoy blips on enemy radar within 620 of it. Decoys move every 5 seconds, to spots taken from a hash of the generator and the time rather than the simulation's random numbers, since they are only ever shown. Deep scan strips stealth, cloaks, and decoys where it reaches, and sight shows a decoy is not there. The commander has 480 of deep scan built in, and with the Thermal Camouflage enhancement (Field Enhancements, above) a cloak, switched with O (when nothing selected can be paused) or its button, that hides it from sight and radar: an enemy cannot see or target it unless it has deep scan there or a unit of its own within 100. A cloak that cannot be paid for switches off. Intel upkeep is paid after shields, in `SimId` order, and a structure that cannot be paid for switches off, showing "off: no energy", until it can. Pausing switches it off without cost. Touching power generators cut upkeep by up to a quarter, as for shields. Only cloaks change what can be targeted, so radar is for seeing: to shoot at a blip, attack the ground there, which is also how decoys draw fire onto nothing. Zoomed out, the outer edge of the viewer's radar coverage, and inside it its deep scan, is drawn as lines: each sensor's circle, less what lies inside another's. The keys are `[` for the Radar and `]` for the Stealth Field Generator. Stealth and jamming share one structure so that both fit in the last keys. The AI builds radar and meets ground blips near its base (AI Defenses and Intel, below), so decoys can draw it; it builds no stealth.
* **Tech Tiers:** T1 engineers and the commander build T1 structures. The T2 land factory builds T2 engineers (12 build power), and upgrades in its queue to the T3 land factory (3,200 materiel, 90 build power), which also builds T3 engineers (30 build power). T2 engineers add the Shield Generator and the tactical missile launcher and defense. T3 engineers add the strategic missile launcher and defense. The commander reaches T2 and T3 through its fabrication packs, which are field enhancements (Field Enhancements, below): its list holds the T3 structures, and what it can build is what is on its list up to its tier, or its suite's. From the moment a suite is started, what it unlocks can be ordered: such a build is queued behind the pack whether or not Shift is held, so it never cancels the pack, and starts once the pack is done. A pack that is cancelled takes the structures queued for its tier with it. A structure's build power drives only its own upgrades, and never makes it a builder. T2 and T3 builders also build the higher tiers of the basic structures directly, at what the upgrade costs: the T2 and T3 materiel extractors, the T2 and T3 power generators (500 and 2,500 energy a second, 3 by 3 and 5 by 5, blowing up for 1,500 within 100 and 5,500 within 200), the T2 radar and the deep scan array, and the T2 point defense (210 every 1.7 seconds within 40 of where it lands, out to 1,000: three shells of 70 as one). The radar now upgrades to the T2 radar (radar 4,000, 150 energy a second), which upgrades to the deep scan array. Factories upgraded to T2 or T3 are headquarters. T2 and T3 builders also build support factories, for far less (the T2 land one 580 materiel, the T3 one 1,440; the T2 air one 510, the T3 one 1,510), but only while the team has a finished headquarters of that kind and at least that tier. A support factory builds nothing above the tier of the team's best headquarters of its kind, and upgrades from T2 to T3 only with a T3 headquarters: without one, such an item waits in its queue at no cost, and the build menu leaves it out. With factories of two tiers selected, the upgrade button is for the lowest tier, and only the factories that upgrade to the same thing take it. The AI climbs to T3 (§18.4, AI Tech and Economy).
* **Field Enhancements:** Enhancements are data, in `[[enhancement]]` tables beside the units, each with its slot, cost, build time, the enhancement it builds on, and its effects, which are totals over the unit's own blueprint, not steps. The commander's ten: in the weapons bay, the Heavy Cannon (800 materiel, 24,000 energy; the gun's range from 440 to 600 and damage from 70 to 140); in the fabrication bay, the T2 Fabrication Pack (800 materiel, 21,000 energy, 1,000 build time; 42 build power, T2 structures, 2,000 more health, healing 10 a second), the T3 Fabrication Pack, which builds on it (2,400 materiel, 50,000 energy, 8,400; 100 build power, T3, 3,000 more health, 20 a second); and in the systems bay, the Damage Control (800 materiel, 24,000 energy; 1,500 more health, 40 a second), the Field Shield (2,300 materiel, 112,500 energy; a dome of radius 30 with 19,000 health, 250 energy a second), the Area Shield (1,400 materiel, 40,000 energy; radius 200, 5,000 health, 200 a second), Signature Suppression (650 materiel, 14,000 energy; off enemy radar for 50 energy a second, 2,000 more health), the Thermal Camouflage, which builds on it (6,000 materiel, 750,000 energy; the §9 cloak for 3,000 a second while on, 12,000 more health), the Orbital Redeploy (15,000 materiel, 1,500,000 energy), and the Onboard Reactor (5,000 materiel, 175,000 energy; 14 materiel and 2,500 energy a second). Bays follow §2.1. A commander has one enhancement in each bay. Starting one removes what is in its slot at once, and that is lost, unless the new one builds on it, which stays until the new one is done. One that builds on another includes it: with the T3 pack, the T2 one shows as there and cannot be taken again, which would only be a step back, and the same goes for stealth under the cloak. Their effects replace each other, so the T3 pack's 3,000 health is all it adds, not 3,000 on top of 2,000. It works like an upgrade in place: the commander stands still, puts all its build power into it, engineers can assist, and a new order cancels it, losing what was spent. Health added comes with the enhancement and goes with it, and veterancy adds its share on top. Shields from enhancements are ordinary domes that move with the commander. The commander no longer has a cloak built in: §2.1 puts it in the systems bay. **Orbital Redeploy** (Ctrl+T, then a click) needs the enhancement, and a spot that is open ground in sight or on the team's radar or deep scan. It costs, worked out in units a twentieth of ours, 100,000 energy plus half the distance to the power 2.1, up to 2,500,000, over 20 seconds plus a hundred-thousandth of the distance to the power 2.1, up to 50. The power is worked out in whole numbers, to 1/2048, so that every machine agrees. The energy is paid evenly every tick, after shields and intel, and the charge aborts on the first tick it cannot be paid, as §2.1 asks. The commander stands still while charging, and a new order cancels it. It lands on the spot, and any enemy mobile unit it lands on is destroyed. A ring shows where, filling as it charges. **Self-destruct** (Ctrl+K) counts down 5 seconds for any of the player's units, and pressing it again stops the count. The unit then dies with its death explosion, the commander's reactor included, which ends the match, and it leaves no debris. The enhancements are buttons under a collapsible heading in the command panel, slot by slot, with their costs and what they do, since there are no keys left. The AI does not use them.
* **Field Rigs:** The Rig Yard (T3, 3,000 materiel, 30,000 energy, 4 by 4, 10,000 health, 120 build power) is built by T3 engineers, field rigs, and the commander with its T3 pack, and builds the Field Rig (2,100 materiel, 25,200 energy, 14,400 build time, so 120 seconds at the gateway alone). It has 16,000 health, 56 build power, the T3 engineer's list and the gateway, a gun with three times the commander's fire (300 a second, range 500), 1 materiel and 20 energy a second, 275 materiel and 500 energy of storage, and 320 of deep scan. Its death sets off a small blast, 1,000 within 200, and does not end the match: only the commander's does. Mobile units' income and storage now count for any builder, not only the commander. Its enhancements follow §2.1's slots, as the commander's do, so both weapon upgrades are in the weapons bay and only one can be had at a time: the Long Barrel (800 materiel; range 700 and a 40 blast) and the Capacitor Bank (1,000 materiel; twice the rate of fire); in the fabrication bay, the Rapid Fabricator (800 materiel; 98 build power); and in the systems bay, the Sensor Mast (600 materiel; sight 900 and deep scan 1,600), the Field Shield (26,000 health, 500 energy a second), the Area Shield built on it (radius 200, 52,000 health, 1,000 a second), the Radar Jammer (300 materiel; 5 decoys within 520 for 100 energy a second, as the stealth field generator's decoys work), the Orbital Redeploy (15,000 materiel, 21,000 build time), and the Onboard Reactor (10 materiel and 1,000 energy a second). Ready-made loadouts, field rigs built with enhancements on, are not built. The AI does not build rig yards.
* **Materiel Extractor Upgrades:** The T1 extractor (36 materiel, 400 health) makes 2 materiel a second for 2 energy a second, and upgrades in place with U, at its own build power of 10, to T2 (900 materiel and 5,400 energy for the upgrade, 90 seconds; 2,000 health, 6 materiel for 9 energy), which upgrades at its 15 to T3 (4,600 materiel and 31,625 energy, about 192 seconds; 7,000 health, 18 materiel for 54 energy). Like factories, the higher tiers are reached only by upgrading. An upgrading extractor keeps making materiel at its old rate, and engineers can assist. Extractors and building share the energy left after shields, intel, and teleports: when a team cannot pay for both, every extractor makes the same share of its materiel, and everything being built goes at that same share, instead of extractors switching off in turn or being paid first. Paid first, they took all of a raided team's energy, so it could never build another generator, and a soak match on Twin Passes (seed 106) stood still from 17 minutes to the time limit. The resource bar shows what they really make. A paused extractor makes and spends nothing, and generators beside one cut its upkeep as they do other structures'. The AI upgrades its extractors to T2 and T3 (§18.4, AI Tech and Economy).
* **Mirrored Paths:** A span where two sectors meet is entered at its middle cell, and a span an even number of cells long has two. It takes the one nearer the middle of the map, so that a span and its image turned 180 degrees pick cells that are images of each other, and mirrored units walk mirrored paths. It used to take the one at the higher index, which sent a mirrored engineer a few units off its partner's path.
* **One Scale:** Every range and radius is on one scale, where the slice had set some of its own. Sight, radar, deep scan, stealth and shield radii, weapon ranges and blasts, missile defense ranges, and build ranges are set together: the commander sees 520 and has 480 of deep scan, the light bot sees 360 and reaches 280, the tactical missile launcher reaches 5,120, and the radar covers 2,320. Movement speeds, projectile speeds, and unit sizes keep the slice's own values for now: converting them needs gravity matched for artillery arcs, and changes how fights feel rather than how ranges compare. Minimum ranges were not built then (see Artillery). The stealth field generator's decoys scale with its field, to 620. The commander's death blast keeps the 300 set above.
* **Selecting Fighters:** A box, a double-click, or Ctrl+A over units that can fight and units that cannot selects only those that can fight: units with a weapon, a shield, or a missile or torpedo defense, the commander, mobile shields, escort corvettes, and missile submarines included, and air scouts, which go with an army to find its targets; but not engineers and others that build structures, transports, nor structures. (Counting anything with build power as an engineer, and only armed units as fighters, left the missile submarines and corvettes out of a box over the fleet.) With no fighters among them, it selects the units, and with no units, the structures. Ctrl+A selects all of the player's units this way.
* **Builders That Cannot Build It:** Told to build something, the builders in the selection that cannot, such as T1 engineers given a T2 structure, drop what they were doing and help the first builder that can, rather than going on with their old orders. Before, a selection of the commander and an engineer building an air factory, told to build a T2 power generator, left the engineer on the factory, which went on going up beside the generator.
* **Capture:** Builders that can walk, engineers, the commander, and field rigs, capture an enemy unit or structure in half its build time at their combined build power, for its energy cost, which an energy stall slows like any work. Progress is kept on the target and lost if every capturer stops, so a capture that is interrupted starts over. The captured unit changes sides as it is, with its health, veterancy, and enhancements, and with nothing to do: its orders and a factory's queue, repeat, and rally point are cleared, a pause or self-destruct is dropped, and units that were shooting it stop. Its income and storage go to its new team. Commanders and field rigs cannot be captured, nor can structures still being built. Engineers repairing a unit stop once it is captured from them. Right-clicking an enemy has selected armed units attack it and builders that cannot fight capture it; Ctrl+C arms a capture for any builder, the commander included. A capture shows as a work beam, and in the plan as a support step. The AI does not capture.
* **Materiel Fabrication and Geothermal Power:** The fabricators are T2 and T3 rather than §3.3's T1 and T3, and the plant makes 100 energy a second, 5 generators' worth, rather than §3.4's 10 to 12. The Materiel Fabricator (T2, 200 materiel, 4,000 energy, 360 health, 1 by 1) makes 1 materiel a second for 100 energy a second, and the T3 one (4,000 materiel, 120,000 energy, 6,000 health, 3 by 3) 16 for 1,500. Fabricators draw their energy with the extractors, so a stall slows both by the same share, and generators beside them cut it. One destroyed while running, that is finished, not paused, and with energy to run on, blows up: 370 damage within 100, or 5,000 within 200. The Geothermal Plant (T1, 160 materiel, 800 energy, 1,800 health, 3 by 3) makes 100 energy a second, and is built with a geothermal vent under the middle of its footprint. Vents are part of the map, like deposits, and no other structure may cover one. Maps from files keep their vents, and both bundled maps have them: one near each base, and more out on the map to contest. The AI builds a plant on the free vent nearest home, within 2,500, when its energy runs short, before any generator; it builds no fabricators. Placing a plant marks the free vents and snaps to them, as placing an extractor does deposits, and vents are drawn on the ground.
* **Bundled Maps:** Five maps come with the game, this game's own so that they can be shipped (§20), generated by its own code: `--map highland`, `--map twin_passes`, `--map saddleback` (§17.3, added 2026-11-22), and `--map stillwater` and `--map scattered_isles` (§17.3, added 2026-11-24). Each is generated in the simulation from a fixed seed with whole-number math, so it is the same map on every machine and in every match, and only the match's own seed varies the play. Both turn into themselves about the map's center, as the AI's base plan does. The hills are value noise averaged with their own turned image, because copying one half onto the other leaves a seam of false cliffs down the middle row; features are then placed in pairs. Highland is 10 km: a plateau of rolling hills, steep-sided outcrops and short ridges kept clear of the bases, about 1,500 rocks in fields and 200 trees, 46 deposits, and starts in opposite corners. Twin Passes is 20 km: a sea down the middle whose west coast wanders, with the east coast its turned image, crossed by two land bridges at 32% and 68% of the way with two deposits on each, forests on both shores (about 1,600 trees), 78 deposits, and starts facing each other across the water. Cells steeper than 0.75 of height per unit of ground, or under the water, are blocked, as on maps from files. In 24 AI matches each, Highland went 15 to 9 and Twin Passes 11 to 12 with a draw, with no replay mismatches, and tick p99 2.7 ms and 5.3 ms. Tests check each map turns into itself, that the starts are joined by land, that every deposit is on open ground, and that the bridges cross. Ground under the water level is drawn as water, darker with depth, and the placement preview now knows steep and flooded cells as well as walls. `examples/map_picture.rs` draws a bundled map from above, for designing them.
* **T1 Land, Re-derived:** The Medium Tank, Light Walker, Light Artillery, and Land Scout are this game's own numbers, derived from `data/balance_targets.toml` by `--balance-derive t1` and priced by duels (BALANCE.md; reports/balance/2026-11-15-t1-land.md), replacing the starting numbers the entries above quote. The Medium Tank is the anchor: 40 materiel, 420 health, 30 a second out to 350, speed 7.2, sight 385. The Light Walker is the raider: 24 materiel, 151 health, 21.6 a second out to 210, speed 13. Light Artillery is now artillery, with a least range of half its 595: 48 materiel, 451 health, 140 within 16 every 5 seconds, shells 2 seconds in flight. The Land Scout is cheap sight: 12 materiel, 40 health, speed 14.4, sight 695. At equal materiel, Medium Tanks beat Light Walkers keeping half their army; attack-moving, they lose to Light Artillery, which keeps two thirds of its own, and spread out and charging they win keeping three fifths; massed Light Walkers lose attack-moving too, and since shells came down where they were aimed (2026-11-21, reports/balance/2026-11-21-artillery-arcs.md), only come out about even with micro: raiders are no longer a counter of artillery, which shells a charge from the front, but finish it off when they reach it left alone. Ten Medium Tanks against ten take 30 seconds.
* **T2 Land, Re-derived:** The Heavy Tank, Grenadier Walker, Assault Hovercraft, and Mobile Missile Launcher are this game's own numbers, derived from `data/balance_targets.toml` by `--balance-derive t2` against T1 land frozen, and priced by duels (BALANCE.md; reports/balance/2026-11-20-t2-land.md), replacing the numbers the entries above and below quote. The Heavy Tank is the T2 line unit: 120 materiel, three Medium Tanks, 1,381 health, 103 every 1.2 seconds out to 438, speed 6.2, sight 480, about as strong per materiel as the Medium Tank (1.07 times), winning by range and toughness; ten against ten take 35 seconds. The Grenadier Walker is a splash skirmisher: 144 materiel, 1,131 health, 162 within 19 every 2 seconds out to 529, a fifth past the Heavy Tank, at its speed; it beats Heavy Tanks that attack-move into it, keeping two fifths of its army, and loses to them spread out and charging, and Light Artillery, which outranges it, comes out about even with it. The Assault Hovercraft is tough, fast, and amphibious: 240 materiel, 3,129 health, 179 within 10 every 1.6 seconds out to 438, at the Medium Tank's speed of 7.2, about even with the Heavy Tank for its materiel. The Mobile Missile Launcher is siege: 180 materiel, 1,353 health, a cruise missile of 420 every 5 seconds on its own out to 1,295 and no closer than 259. Since 2026-11-21 it is priced at 0.8 of T1 artillery's strength per materiel (`[missiles]`) rather than by a duel against it: artillery is the better unit, as missiles miss what keeps moving and missile defense stops them, though the launcher's reach outranges point defenses. Light Artillery beats it, and so does what keeps moving, Heavy Tanks and a few raiders alike; twelve of it break a shield over three T2 point defenses in 21 seconds, losing nothing.
* **T3 Land, Re-derived:** The Heavy Walker, Siege Walker, and Mobile Heavy Artillery are this game's own numbers, derived from `data/balance_targets.toml` by `--balance-derive t3` against T1 and T2 land frozen, and priced by duels (BALANCE.md; reports/balance/2026-11-21-t3-land.md), replacing the numbers the entries above and below quote. The Heavy Walker is the T3 line unit: 360 materiel, three Heavy Tanks, 4,292 health under its personal shield of 700, 71 every 0.3 seconds out to 525, speed 6.1, sight 580, about as strong per materiel as the Heavy Tank (1.07 times); ten against ten take 44 seconds. The Siege Walker is heavy: 720 materiel, 11,010 health, 1,430 within 12 every 4 seconds out to 578, speed 4.3, about even with the Heavy Walker for its materiel. Mobile Heavy Artillery is for sieges, a glass cannon since its blast went wide (see Snipes and Heavy Splash): 600 materiel, 1,300 health, 859 within 83 every 11.5 seconds out to 1,680, a fifth past the T3 Heavy Point Defense, and no closer than 420, shells 3 seconds in flight, with a spread of 60. It sets up for a second before it fires, and any move packs it up. Twenty-six of it, the position's cost, break a heavy shield over three heavy point defenses in 21 seconds and lose nothing. Until 2026-11-24 it fired every 6 seconds; it was slowed so that one of the battleship's three turrets does twice its damage a second, and one and a half times a T2 Artillery Installation's (a test holds both). It is a siege piece, dearer than the line walker, and loses to what reaches it: Heavy Walkers, Heavy Tanks, and Assault Hovercraft that attack-move into it bunched up are still wrecked, but a swarm of Medium Tanks of its cost now overruns it, keeping about half, Siege Walkers edge it, and units spread out and charging, and raiders, which its inaccuracy misses, beat it clearly. (At the slower rate it no longer won the walk-in fight against bunched Medium Tanks that a test had asked of it; for a day its cost was halved to keep that, then the cost was put back and the requirement dropped.)
* **Snipes and Heavy Splash (2026-11-21):** To play as the inspiration does, splash comes in three sizes (`[splash]` in data/balance_targets.toml), by how many units in a tight formation one hit catches: narrow, about 5 (T1 Light Artillery, Grenadier Walkers, the Discharge); medium, about 9 (tactical bombers: the T1 Attack Bomber's 29 and the Fighter-Bomber's 36); and wide, about 29 (the T2 and T3 Artillery Installations, Mobile Heavy Artillery, and the Strategic Bomber, each 70 to 83). And the snipe weapons kill in exact numbers of hits (`[kills]`): the Strategic Bomber's bomb of 5,200 destroys a Heavy Walker in one pass, a Siege Walker in three, Mobile Heavy Artillery in one, a T2 Artillery Installation (now 7,000 health) in two, and a commander in four; the Tactical Missile Launcher's missile of 10,000 a Field Rig or a commander in two, so missile defense is what keeps a commander safe. The commander, at 18,000 health, up from 12,000, is tough against everything else, and falls only to these (its reactor's blast went up with it, to 18,000, so that it still takes another commander with it); they in turn fall to T3 fighters, SAMs, and missile defense. The T3 Artillery Installation's shell of 11,500 destroys any T3 land unit with a direct hit, but scatters up to 175 over a base; the T2 one's, with a spread of 50, suits small bases. Mobile Heavy Artillery is a glass cannon set the same way: it survives one shell from another of its kind but not two, and its shell kills a Medium Tank.
* **Maps From Data:** A map is whole numbers only, heights, deposits, starts, and scenery, so every instance builds the same world from it; the bundled maps (Bundled Maps, above) generate theirs in code, and `--map` picks one for every mode: playing, `--headless`, `--autotest`, and `--soak`. The AI takes its home and the enemy's from the map's starts, and its base plan is Blue's, turned 180° for Red, so maps should mirror. On a map with cliffs in nearly every sector, rebuilding the pathfinding graph after each structure searched every sector, and ticks reached 46 ms; each sector now keeps the walking distances found inside it until a structure goes up or comes down in it, which brought them back to 3.65 ms with identical results. Finding the walkable cell nearest an order's goal now visits only each ring's own cells, with the same winner (see Mirror Fairness for how ties go), which took a 20 km map with a sea from 2.49 ms to 0.34 ms a tick. Reading maps made for other games was removed on 2026-11-15 (PROVENANCE.md).
* **Missiles:** A missile flies itself to a spot: up to its cruise height above the ground, along the ground at that height, which takes it over walls and hills that stop shells, and down a straight line over the last three cruise heights to just below the spot. It climbs no steeper than 45 degrees, and never hits friends on the way. Missile launchers build their missiles, keep them up to a stock limit, and fire only on order: they never pick a target themselves, and building stops, costing nothing, while a silo is full. A right-click on an enemy orders the selected launchers to fire at it, and attack ground (I) at a spot. The Tactical Missile Launcher (450 materiel, tier 2) builds tactical missiles in its queue, at 140 materiel and 2,000 energy, in 30 seconds at its own 10 build power, and keeps up to 6. It fires one 1,500-damage cruise missile, with a 30-unit blast, every 15 seconds while it has any, at 18 units a tick, cruising at 60, to anywhere within 5,120, and goes on firing at its target until the target is gone or it has none left. Shields stop tactical missiles, which is what they are for: four collapse a dome. The Tactical Missile Defense (200 materiel) shoots down the nearest enemy cruise missile within 620 of it, instantly, then reloads for 3 seconds. A missile crosses that in about 34 ticks, so it gets two shots at a volley, and a third missile gets through. The Strategic Missile Launcher (16,500 materiel and 412,500 energy, tier 3, 6 by 6, build power 1,500) builds nuclear missiles in its queue, like a factory, at 12,000 materiel and 1,500,000 energy each, 300 seconds apiece, and keeps up to 5. Each Attack or attack-ground order fires one, anywhere on the map, at 25 a tick, cruising at 500. Its blast has two rings: 70,000 damage within 600, which kills anything, commanders included, and 500 more within 800, which kills light units and structures. It hits friend and foe, and clears debris. Shields do not stop it. The Strategic Missile Defense (1,000 materiel, 2 by 2) builds interceptors the same way, at 500 materiel and 6,000 energy, keeps up to 7, and fires one at any enemy nuclear missile that comes within 1,800 of it across the ground, wherever the missile is headed; it flies up to meet the missile and never misses (Strategic Interceptors, below). Defenses act before anything moves each tick, in `SimId` order, and never shoot the same missile twice. A tactical missile shot down simply vanishes, with a flash from the defense to it. The panel shows how many missiles or interceptors a silo holds. The AI builds tactical missiles and their defense, but no nuclear ones yet (AI Defenses and Intel, below).
* **Shields:** The Shield Generator (300 materiel, 3,000 energy, 2 by 2) holds a dome of radius 260 with 5,000 health, which heals 50 a second, about what one heavy tank does to it. It is tier 2. The dome is a sphere around the generator's base. An enemy shell that enters it from outside stops there and does all its damage to the dome, so nothing under it is hit, and a blast does not go off beneath. A shell that starts inside flies out freely, so units under a dome fire out, and units under an enemy dome can still fire at what is there with them. A team's own shells always pass its domes. A splash shell that strikes a dome does its damage to every other enemy dome within its blast radius of where it struck, so overlapping domes all take it (§8.1). Discharge does at most its structure damage to a dome. A dome that runs out collapses and comes back at full strength after 30 seconds. It costs 30 energy a second while up, less a quarter at most from touching power generators, as factories get. Upkeep is paid first, in `SimId` order, and a dome the team cannot pay for collapses, so an energy stall brings shields down one by one. Pausing a generator (O) drops its dome and its upkeep, keeping its health, and resuming raises it at once. A generator under construction has no dome. The command panel shows a selected generator's shield strength, or how long until it is back. Domes are drawn see-through in the team's color, brighter at the rim and fainter as they weaken. The AI does not build shields yet.
* **Wreck Blasting:** Debris has half the health of the unit it came from, scaled by how much of it had been built: 100 for a light bot, 450 for a heavy tank. A shell that comes down on bare ground hits any debris it lands on, and a blast hits all debris within its radius, including a destroyed unit's death explosion. Debris loses materiel and energy in the same proportion as health, and is gone with what is left when its health runs out, so nobody gets that materiel. Shells do not hit debris on the way, only where they come down, so debris never shields units behind it. Trees and rocks are not harmed, so the map's starting reclaim cannot be shot away. Attack ground (I, then a click) makes armed units, structures included, fire at a spot until they are given another order, walking until it is in range. It works out of sight, the spot stays in the unit's plan under fog of war, and it takes over the gun, so the unit fires at nothing else. Clicking debris aims at its middle. The shell aims a sixteenth of a unit below the ground: rounding could otherwise leave it just above the ground at the spot, to skim on, and any deeper makes a flat shot land short. The AI does not use it yet.
* **Sinking and Falling Wrecks (2026-11-26):** Every sea has a floor, 50 below the water, that its shores and islands rise from (`SEA_DEPTH_UNITS` in `terrain.rs`). Seabed walkers walk on it, and `tests/map_test.rs` checks it on every map with water. A wreck left above the ground, by a ship or an aircraft, comes down before it settles. In the air it keeps fifteen sixteenths of the way it was going each tick and falls as a bomb does, so a downed aircraft comes down well ahead of where it was hit: an interceptor about 380 ahead, two seconds later. In the water it keeps three quarters of its way across each tick and sinks, faster and faster up to 6 a second, so a ship slips under in a few seconds and lies on the floor about nine seconds after it was lost; an aircraft that falls into the sea goes down the same way. A wreck that falls into the water loses the share a sunken wreck loses (§6.1), as a ship's does. While it falls, a wreck cannot be reclaimed or blasted, and a builder sent to reclaim it waits for it to land. On the floor it is reclaimed like any other: a builder's reach is measured across the ground, not up and down, so an engineer floating over a wreck reclaims it, and a right-click on the water over one orders that. How a wreck moves is part of the checksum only while one is coming down, so the battle's golden checksums did not change; the AI match's did (`tests/golden/ai_match.txt`), since its aircraft now come down where they fall. `tests/sinking_test.rs` covers it. As drawn, a downed aircraft burns and trails black smoke as it falls, nose down along the way it falls and rolling, and crashes in a burst of fire and dirt on land, leaving a scorch, or in a splash of spray at sea. A ship goes down bow first and listing, with smoke and foam as it goes under and bubbles after, and a slick of oil spreads on the water over every wreck on the sea floor, to show where it lies, since the water hides the floor. `--autotest` with `--look-at <x>,<y>,<altitude>` and `--capture` holds the camera over one spot and saves pictures of the world alone, for screenshots of a fight.
* **Interface Look (2026-11-26):** Panels are plain: square corners, one steel rim, and a thin brass line along the top, over the same dark fill (`hud::panel_shapes`). They used to have cut corners with lights at each cut and a cyan line inside the rim. Buttons are flat, and hovered or chosen ones light up brass where they lit up cyan, in egui's own widgets too. The score panel is slimmer while playing: the clock, then the map (cut short when long) and your units on one line, then a line for each side with its materiel income a second and its army's worth; the column headings and the map's own line went, and how a match ended follows a side's name. Watching keeps the full table. The red target is four corner brackets round the spot, with short ticks pointing in and a dot, where it was a ring and cross; it shows exactly where it did. The minimap is lettered A to H across the top and numbered 1 to 8 down the left, with faint lines between the squares (`minimap::grid_square`), so that a spot on the map can be named; north is up, so A1 is the far left corner. Hotkeys and where every panel sits are unchanged.
* **Trees and Rocks:** The skirmish map has 10 forests of 30 trees and 8 fields of 5 rocks on each half, each with its mirror image on the other, clear of the bases, the deposits, and the walls. A tree holds 20 energy and 1 materiel, and a rock 5 materiel for each unit of its radius, which is 5 to 10, so 25 to 50. Reclaim takes materiel at 2 and energy at 10 a second for each unit of build power, both at once, so an engineer clears a tree in under half a second and a large rock in 5 seconds. They do not block movement or building: a structure placed over them clears them, and its team gets what they held, as though its builder had reclaimed them first. Debris under a new structure stays, so that a cheap building on a graveyard cannot take its materiel at once. Trees and rocks are part of the map, so they show through fog of war, for the AI as for the player, and its idle engineers reclaim them on their own side. Shooting does not destroy them (see Wreck Blasting).
* **Formation Moves:** Ctrl+right-click, or J and a click, gives a formation move, and Alt makes it an attack-move. A plain right-click stays a plain move. A leader point walks the group's route at nine tenths of the slowest member's speed, so that it can catch up after a bend, and each member keeps to its own spot in a grid that moves with the leader. The grid is the one the group ends in, and turns towards where the leader is heading, no faster than half the pace at its outer spots. The leader waits for members still finishing earlier orders, so a queued formation move sets off together, and for up to 10 seconds for members more than four spacings out of place. On an attack-move it waits while any member is firing at a target in range, so the group stops together. A member chasing a target out of range does not hold it, since a chase can go on for as long as the target runs: the group goes on, and the member catches up once the target is destroyed. A member whose spot is blocked, or more than twelve spacings away, follows the route on its own until it is back in range, and near the goal members leave for their own spots, as in any group order. A formation ends when fewer than two members are left on it. The AI's attack waves do not use formations: at the slowest unit's pace they arrived late enough that, in 200 AI-vs-AI matches, 6 ran past the time limit, against none with each unit at its own speed.
* **Blocked Shots:** A unit does not fire at a target it picked for itself if the ground would stop the shell, which it checks by flying the shell's arc against the terrain. It passes over that target for 5 seconds, so it looks for another or walks on, and an attack-move routes around the hill or wall. A target the player ordered is fired at regardless.
* **AI Waves:** A wave grows by a quarter each time, up to 50 units, but an army that has not grown for a minute goes with what it has, at least 10 units, since a stalled economy may never field the next wave. With nothing in sight, a full-size wave attacks the enemy's base, and smaller waves mostly raid deposits more than 2,000 units from it, away from its defenses. Between equal AIs on a fair map, a defended base beats an equal wave, so without raids to open up a lead, some matches went on past 67 minutes. Gathering each wave short of the enemy's base before attacking was tried and made this worse. After 35 minutes, the commander goes with each full-size wave, which either breaks the enemy's base or loses the commander, and ends the match either way. Waves are never sent at what is under the water, where tanks cannot hit it, and the commander goes only with a wave headed for land: two commanders that followed their waves onto a seabed once stalked each other there, neither able to fire, until the match ran out of time. Once trees and rocks had raised both incomes, an even game could otherwise go on past the limit, with neither commander ever coming under fire.
* **The Full Line:** The rest of the land, naval, and defensive roster, with salvo weapons as one shot at a time at the same damage a second. Land: the T1 Land Scout (fast and fragile, with radar 880 out and a pea-shooter) and Medium Tank (see T1 Land, Re-derived); the T2 Grenadier Walker (see T2 Land, Re-derived), Mobile Shield Generator (a 170-wide dome of 3,000, for 80 energy a second), and Combat Engineer (a T2 builder of forward defenses, artillery, intel, and T1 factories, 1,040 health, with radar and a gun); the T3 Heavy Walker (a personal shield over a gatling) and Siege Walker (one big cannon; both see T3 Land, Re-derived). Naval: the T2 Torpedo Boat (2,800 health since 2026-11-24, about 125 torpedo damage a second out to 1,100, sonar, and torpedo decoys). Structures: the T3 Heavy Point Defense (6,500 health, a gatling of about 625 a second out to 1,400, two and a half times its earlier 250 since 2026-11-25, so that three hold against half again their cost in Heavy Walkers, `[premiums]`; its health is unchanged, so sieges by what outranges it take as long), the T3 Heavy Shield Generator (a dome 440 out of 17,000, for 400 energy a second), and the Wall Section (3 materiel, 500 health, 1 by 1, blocking movement). Bots of each tier share a symbol and a key (E), and so do tanks (C), with the Heavy Tank moving from E to C and the Assault Hovercraft to V. The AI builds Medium Tanks at T1, Grenadier Walkers and Mobile Shields at T2, the T3 bots at T3, and Torpedo Boats in its navy, and keeps at most two Mobile Shields, none while its energy is short. In an energy crisis it now pauses its factories and everything with upkeep, shields and radar included, along with its extractors: in one test a shield and a radar took all of what little energy came in, so no new generator ever finished. Air transports carry land units (see Air Transports).
* **Air Transports:** The T1 Light Air Transport (120 materiel, 500 health) carries 6 small, 2 medium, or 1 large; the T2 Air Transport (330 materiel, 1,675 health, with a light gun) 14, 6, or 3; and the T3 Heavy Air Transport (1,400 materiel, 4,500 health, a shield of 3,000, and a gun) 28, 12, or 6. T1 land units are small, T2 medium, and T3 and field rigs large; a mixed load shares the room, each unit taking its share of it. Aircraft, ships, structures, and commanders cannot be carried (a carried commander would look destroyed to the rule that ends the match). Right-clicking a finished transport with land units selected sends them to board it (`Load`): they follow it, it flies to the middle of them and settles, and each gets in once within 40 of it, past both radii, while there is room; any other order calls boarding off, and a unit left with no room stops. Inside, a unit is out of the world's unit list, so nothing can see, target, or order it, and it neither moves nor fires; it rides at its transport's position, keeping its health, rank, and enhancements, and records which of the world's lists it was in. Ctrl+U arms Unload: the transport flies to the spot and, once within 80, sets everything down on the nearest free cells it can stand on, up to 12 cells out, back into the lists it left; anything with nowhere to stand stays on board. A transport sent elsewhere first forgets the unload. Its cargo goes down with it, leaving wreckage. The selection panel says how many it carries. Transports are on Z at the air factory. The AI does not use them yet.
* **Field Workshops:** The T2 Field Workshop (525 materiel, 1,000 health, 2 by 2) flies a drone that helps, by itself, whatever friendly construction, production, or upgrade is within 300, nearest first, and failing that repairs the nearest damaged friend there: 20 build power. It upgrades to the T3 station (2,500 health), which flies two, for 30 (25 and 50 before). Every second it looks for work if it has none, and gives up a factory once its queue is empty, so it moves on. Work beyond its reach it lets go, since it cannot walk there. It is on V, with airfield and walls. Its beam shows like a builder's. The AI puts them beside its land factories (AI Tech and Economy).
* **Unit Data Audit:** Every unit and structure was compared, field by field, with the source of its starting values (PROVENANCE.md), and the unit data now follows it wherever it differed: health (the Light Walker's 60, the Heavy Tank's 1,500, the T1, T2, and T3 Land Factories' 4,000, 8,000, and 16,000, the T1 Radar's 10, the Shield Generator's 250, the commander's 12,000), costs and build times (engineers 260, 650, and 1,560; the T2 factory upgrade 2,300; the T3 headquarters 5,220 materiel, 47,400 energy, and 12,000; the Shield Generator 600 materiel, 6,000 energy, 1,150; the strategic missile launcher, defense, and missiles, which are an order of magnitude dearer than ours were), speeds (the Light Walker 8.4, Light Artillery 5.6, Heavy Tank 6.2, commander 3.4, engineers 3.8), sight and sensors (structures 400, the Air Scout's sonar 560), and weapons where the comparison held (the Light Walker 7 every 0.3 seconds, T1 Point Defense 150 every 0.3 since 2026-11-25, with 3,900 health, three times what they were, so that it holds against half again its cost in tanks (`[premiums]`), Light Artillery 100 every 8.3 at 28, the Heavy Air Transport's anti-air racks). Structure footprints are set so that a T1 generator is 1 by 1 and adjacency touches the same neighbors: factories are 4 by 4, naval factories 6 by 6, the Shield Generator and Stealth Field Generator 3 by 3, the strategic missile launcher 6 by 6. Where an earlier entry quotes other numbers, the unit data is right. The T1 Attack Bomber already matched: four bombs of 42.5 and 45 of burning, 350 a pass, splash 60; it was the T1 factory's 3,200 health, 20% too low, that made it fall fast. The Fighter-Bomber matched too, but under fog it never bombed: from its height and speed it lets go about 800 short of its target, beyond its own sight of 640, and it would only drop on what it saw, so bombers now drop on what their team has on radar or deep scan as well, which is why it carries a radar of 1,280. With the new costs the AI fell apart: it upgraded its only factory to T3, which now takes minutes, and was left with no army, and it never built a second factory while starved of materiel. It now always has a second land factory, upgrades a factory only while another goes on making units, waits for 50 materiel a second (was 30) before T3, and finishes every structure left unfinished, not only those of 500 materiel or more, which had left its energy storage half built and Discharge out of reach.
* **Factory Exits:** Factories build in the open, so what they make can be watched taking shape. A land factory is an assembly bay, a floor between two long walls under a gantry crane, open at its east and west ends; an air factory is a deck with a round pad, a hangar, and a tower; a naval factory is a dock, two piers with a slip of open water between them, open at both ends. While a unit is built it stands in the bay, on the pad, or afloat in the slip, in its team's colors but unlit, rising from the floor as it nears completion; anyone who can see the factory sees what it is making and how far along (`UnitView::assembling`). A finished unit starts in the bay. A land unit, hovercraft, or ship drives straight out of the open end that faces its rally point, or the map center if there is no rally, at its own speed, facing the way out, and only then sets off for the rally point (`LeavingFactory`). If both ends are blocked it leaves by a side. An order given on the way out takes over once the unit is out, instead of the rally. An aircraft lifts straight off its pad and flies to the rally point, or just clear of the factory if there is none. When the two ends face the rally equally, the one clockwise of it wins, so mirrored factories pick mirrored ends.
* **Aircraft:** An aircraft flies at its own height over land and water, straight for where it is going, with no pathfinding, and bumps into nothing. In flight it never stops: it flies the way it faces and turns at its turn rate, so it moves in arcs. An aircraft with nothing to do lands: it flies straight on as it slows and comes down, over 3 seconds, and rests on the ground until it gets an order or a target, when it takes off again just as gradually. Where there is no open ground below, over water, cliffs, or buildings, or near the map's edge, it circles at its height instead, turning in towards the middle near the edge. A landed aircraft is a ground unit: ground weapons and blasts hit it, anti-air does not, and shots already on their way to it are spent. It bombs only from its flying height. A point inside the circles it would turn in cannot be turned to, so it flies on until it can. A move ends within three ticks' flight of the goal, and a group of aircraft keeps no formation. Attacking, it makes passes: having flown over its target, it flies on for its break-off distance before turning back. A bomber lets its bomb go its throw short of the target, the distance the bomb carries forward as it falls, so it flies on at least far enough to turn round twice its turn radius, cover that throw, and have a second's run-up to settle on the line: it bombs on every pass once reloaded. With a shorter break-off, the T2 fighter-bomber, whose bombs carry 840, turned back 400 past its target, never got back on a line to it, and circled it for good. Only anti-air weapons hit aircraft in flight, and only they can be told to attack one: shots at aircraft steer themselves to them and hit only them, and are spent if the aircraft dies first. Shells and blasts on the ground do not touch aircraft. Aircraft only fight what comes within their weapons' range, and cannot be captured in flight. The Air Factory (T1, 210 materiel, 2,400 energy, 4,000 health, 4 by 4, build power 20) builds the Air Scout (40 materiel, 580 energy, 35 health, speed 38, height 320, vision 840, radar 1,280), the Interceptor (37 materiel, 1,658 energy, 435 health since 2026-11-25, priced and built as the inspiration has it against its T1 tank, from 50, 2,250, and 295; speed 30, height 280, 50 damage a second within 500, at aircraft only), and the Attack Bomber (90 materiel, 2,050 energy, 215 health, speed 20, height 280). The bomber drops one bomb every 5 seconds on ground targets within 800, as it passes over: let go short of the target, the bomb keeps the bomber's speed and falls at ten times the gravity shells use here, and does 350 within 60, a carpet of four napalm bombs and their burning added up. The Anti-Air Turret (T1, 150 materiel, 1,500 energy, 4,500 health, 1 by 1) does 69 every 0.7 seconds within 880 (800 health and 46 until 2026-11-25: static anti-air is now half again as strong per cost against aircraft as mobile, mostly in health, so that fast T3 aircraft still pass over it, `[premiums]`), and the Mobile Anti-Air (55 materiel, 275 energy, 310 health, from land factories) 26 a second within 640: each volley of two shots is one shot here. The AI builds no aircraft yet.
* **Fuel and Airfield:** Each aircraft has its own seconds of fuel (scout 500, interceptor 300, bomber 400), and burns a tick's worth each tick in the air. With none left it flies at half its height and half its speed. On the ground it refuels at a recharge rate of 5, five ticks of fuel each tick. The Airfield (T2, 175 materiel, 2,100 energy, 500 health, 3 by 3) has four pads: a T1 aircraft takes one, a T2 one two, and a T3 one all four. Right-clicking one with aircraft selected sends them to it. Each lands on the first pad free, or circles overhead while they are all taken, and on the pad refuels at 50 times the ground rate and is repaired 500 health a second for 30 energy a second. Once full on both, it leaves the pad, flies clear of the facility, and lands beside it, as an aircraft with nothing to do over any structure flies on to open ground. A new order ends the trip. As §13.2 asks, aircraft on patrol never land, and below a fifth of a tank go to the nearest of their team's facilities, and back to their patrol once refuelled. A facility destroyed or captured leaves its aircraft where they are.

* **Adjacency:** A fixed table of bonuses (PROVENANCE.md). Each finished structure of the team touching another gives it a bonus that depends on the giver and on the receiver's size class: SIZE4 for 1 by 1 structures, SIZE8 for 2 by 2, SIZE12 for 3 by 3, SIZE16 for factories, and SIZE20 for the largest, with set classes where a footprint here differs (the shield and stealth field generators are SIZE12, the T3 power generator SIZE16, and the rig yard SIZE20). Generators take a share off the energy that a factory or an upgrading structure spends, and off upkeep: a T1 generator 1.563% off a factory, 6.25% off a 1 by 1 structure's upkeep and 2.08% off a 3 by 3 one's, a T2 generator or geothermal plant 12.5% off anything, and a T3 one 15.62% off a factory and 18.75% off upkeep. Extractors take a share off the materiel that a factory or upgrading structure spends: 7.5%, 10%, or 12.5% off a factory, by tier. Fabricators do a little of the same. Materiel storage adds 12.5% to an extractor's output, and energy storage 25% to a T1 generator's, 8.33% to a T2 generator's or geothermal plant's, and 6.25% to a T3 one's. Each neighbor counts once, bonuses add up, and nothing caps them but how many neighbors fit, except that no discount reaches 90%. The Energy Storage (T1, 250 materiel, 1,200 energy, 500 health, 1 by 1) holds 10,000 energy, and is on `[`.
* **Artillery:** Artillery lobs its shells in a high arc, rising two fifths of the distance across (no more than 3,000), and they come down steeply on where they were aimed: where the target will be, as every shell leads. A shell takes as long as a flat one would at the same speed across the ground, so the arc changes neither its lead nor its timing. It passes over what stands between, and bursts on the ground where it comes down, hitting whatever is there, the target or not: with the target destroyed or gone, it still lands. Missiles keep their own flight. Artillery cannot hit what is closer than its least range, and its shells land at a random spot within its spread of where they are aimed, drawn from the simulation's own random numbers, so that every replay scatters them the same way. The Artillery Installation (T2, 1,900 materiel, 13,585 energy, 7,000 health, 1 by 1) fires 2,000 within 70 every 20 seconds, from 1,000 out to 2,300, with a spread of 50. The Heavy Artillery Installation (T3, 76,000 materiel, 1,424,000 energy, 15,000 health, 5 by 5) fires 11,500 within 83 every 13.3 seconds, from 3,000 out to 16,500, with a spread of 175: a direct hit destroys any T3 land unit, but it scatters over a base. Their turrets turn slowly, at 6 and 3 degrees a second. An installation's shot costs energy, 1,000 or 16,000, taken all at once as it fires, and it waits until the team has it. Generators beside an installation cut that cost and the time between shots, by the adjacency table: a T2 generator 5% and 6.25% off a 1 by 1 installation. Mobile Heavy Artillery (T3, from T3 land factories; see T3 Land, Re-derived) is the T3 land artillery. Artillery may have to set up before it fires (`setup_ticks`): it fires only once it has stood still that long, and any move of its own packs it up again, so it never fires on the move. A push from a neighbour does not count, and it counts as stopped once it is below an eighth of its speed. Mobile Heavy Artillery sets up in a second; from a standing start it sets up while it finds and aims at its target, so that costs it nothing. The installations are on `]`. The T1 Light Artillery keeps its own numbers for now, and the AI builds none of these.
* **Extra Weapons:** A unit may carry weapons beyond its main one, in `extra_weapons`, each firing shells, or shots that steer to aircraft. Each picks its own target: the one its unit was ordered to attack, if it can hit it, or else the nearest enemy it can hit, mobile units first, on the same cadence as main weapons. Each reloads on its own and turns its own turret, and those that hit the ground fire at a ground attack's spot. A unit can be ordered to attack anything any of its weapons can hit. The strategic bomber and heavy gunship carry their anti-air guns this way, and the Fighter-Bomber (T2: 267 materiel, 9,341 energy, 1,696 health, re-priced on 2026-11-24, from T2 and T3 air factories) a carpet of napalm, twenty bombs of 80 as one of 1,600, and railguns that do 150 a second to aircraft within 600.
* **Ships:** Ships sail water deep enough for them, where each corner of a cell is at least 10 below the water, and float on it. They have their own pathfinding over the water, worked out the same way as on land, made the first time a ship needs it, so that games without ships are unchanged. A group of ships and land units given one order gets a route for each, and a ship sent to land goes to the nearest water. Structures on the water block ships, and structures that go on water, such as the naval factory, need deep water under all of them, and launch their ships onto it. The Naval Factory (T1, 300 materiel, 1,500 energy, 4,500 health, 6 by 6, build power 20, on `\`) builds the Frigate (203 materiel, 2,026 energy, 2,984 health, speed 12, radar 1,600), with a gun of 118 every 1.7 seconds out to 560, and a railgun of 27 a second at aircraft out to 960 (20.8 until aircraft were made to obey orders over open sea, 2026-11-24) (re-priced on 2026-11-24 against the land by the inspiration's ratios, its railgun so that frigates hold their own against an equal cost of T1 bombers: `reports/balance/2026-11-24-t1-navy.md`). Engineers build a naval factory from the shore, within their build range. It upgrades to T2 (2,000 materiel, 10,000 energy, build power 90) and T3 (9,500 materiel, 45,000 energy, build power 150) headquarters, and T2 and T3 builders build naval support factories (1,100 and 2,200 materiel) under the same rules as land and air ones. The Destroyer (T2, 1,658 materiel, 10,231 health, re-priced on 2026-11-24, `reports/balance/2026-11-24-t2-air-navy.md`) has two gauss cannons of 475 every 4 seconds out to 1,200, a railgun at aircraft, and torpedoes of 560 every 4 seconds out to 900, the fleet's hunter of submarines. The Cruiser (T2, 1,473 materiel, 4,358 health, radar 3,040, re-priced on 2026-11-24) fires its cruise missiles on its own, 454 within 40 every 2.5 seconds out to 3,000, which launchers it picks targets for and needs no missiles built for, and has a gauss cannon, SAMs that do 1,279 within 80 every 2 seconds to aircraft out to 1,400, bursting over massed aircraft (a blast of 6 until 2026-11-24), and two of the fleet's tactical missile defense mounts, which between them shoot down a tactical missile within 620 every second. The Battleship (T3, 6,842 materiel, 107,000 health, re-priced on 2026-11-24, `reports/balance/2026-11-24-t3-air-navy.md`) has three gauss turrets of 3,000 every 20 seconds out to 3,000, three shells each as one, four light anti-air guns, as one, and two of the fleet's tactical missile defense mounts, and no anti-torpedo charge: its escorts keep aircraft and torpedoes off it. The Battlecruiser (T3, 5,128 materiel, 35,000 health, re-priced on 2026-11-24) is faster, with two laser cannons, one forward and one aft, of 600 every 2 seconds out to 1,600, each doing twice the damage a second of one of the battleship's three turrets, so that the two out-shoot the battleship, 600 a second to 450, inside a little over half its reach and with half its health (until 2026-11-24 each did 210 every 6.7 seconds), and one of the fleet's tactical missile defense mounts. A laser's beam crosses to its target in a straight line within the tick it is fired: it hits one target, with no blast and no scatter, there is nothing to lead and nothing to dodge, and it strikes the first enemy, shield dome, or rise in the ground on its way, so it is not fired at all across ground that stands in its line, where a shell would arc over. The tactical missile defense is one mount, the same wherever a ship carries it: it shoots down a tactical missile within 620 every 2 seconds. The cruiser and the battleship carry two, and so stop one a second between them, and the battlecruiser one (until 2026-11-23 the cruiser's reached 1,200). The destroyer and battlecruiser also carry torpedoes.
* **Under the Sea:** A submarine sails just under the water, on the same water as ships. Submerged, it is on a layer of its own: only torpedoes reach it, and shells and blasts pass over it. It is hidden from the enemy, sight and radar alike, unless the enemy's sonar covers it: then it is a contact on the map, and in sight, it is in full view and can be shot at, as with radar. Torpedoes steer to their target, ships and submarines only, as shots at aircraft do, and are spent if it dies or is gone. The Attack Submarine (T1, from naval factories, 265 materiel, 1,163 health, sonar 1,200, re-priced on 2026-11-24) fires 185 every 4 seconds out to 640; the Torpedo Bomber (T2, from T2 and T3 air factories, 199 materiel, 1,252 health, sonar 880, re-priced on 2026-11-24) drops five torpedoes of 189 a run, every 10 seconds; the Sonar (T1, 150 materiel, sonar 3,200, 20 energy a second) upgrades to T2 (300 materiel, sonar 5,760, 100 energy a second); and the Torpedo Launchers (T1, 332 materiel, 3,000 health, 454 every 4 seconds out to 1,000, re-priced on 2026-11-24 so that an equal cost of frigates closing on them loses; T2, 796 materiel, 12,000 health, four torpedoes of 227 a volley, each its own, out to 1,200, re-priced on 2026-11-24) go on water. Every ship has sonar too. Torpedoes run at 30, because they steer here rather than speed up, and slower ones could never catch a ship. The sonar and torpedo launchers are on `-` and `=`, which set the volume when nothing selected builds them.
* **Surfacing:** A submarine can be ordered up and down, from a toggle in its command bar. On the surface it rides on the water as a ship does: it is seen and shot at as one, and shells and blasts reach it as well as torpedoes. There the Attack Submarine fires its deck gun, 25 a second out to 640, at whatever it can hit on land or water; under the water the gun is silent. The toggle brings every selected submarine up unless all of them are already up, and then takes them all down. Over water deeper than any unit is tall, shells skimming the surface passed straight through ships, because they were let off unit checks for being far above the seabed: the sea's surface is now the ground that counts.
* **Missile Speeds:** Cruise missiles fly at a top speed of their own: the tactical missile launcher's 28 a tick, the cruiser's and the missile submarine's 32, and the mobile missile launcher's 40. They flew at 18 before, which gave missile defenses nearly twice as long to shoot them down.
* **Firing Randomness:** Guns with a firing randomness land their shots up to 20 times it from where they aim, as artillery's spread does: the battleship's gauss cannons 6, the destroyer's 7, the cruiser's 10, the frigate's 4, the light artillery 10, the flak cannons 40 and 50, the Grenadier Walker's grenades 50, its gatling 2, gunships 4 and 6, and point defenses 2 and 4. Torpedoes, which home in, and bombs, whose carpets are already one blast here, keep straight. Each team draws its scatter from a stream of its own, both seeded alike, and Red's is turned about the map's middle, so a mirrored match stays a mirror image: from one shared stream, whichever side fired first drew first, and the sides parted ways.
* **Turn Rates:** Every unit turns at its own rate, in degrees a second: the commander 90, the field rig 150, engineers 180, 150, and 120 by tier, the light bot 50, light artillery 90, and mobile flak 90. Several were faster before, the light bot seven times so.
* **Crowds and Builders:** A unit that bumps into a settled friend near its goal counts as arrived, so that a crowd covering a rally point does not keep later units walking. A builder on its way to work is the exception: it arrives once its work is in reach. Settled by a crowd short of that, it was given the same walk again on the next tick, and stood beside its friend for the rest of the match, as one commander did for half an hour.
* **Amphibious:** Hovercraft and seabed walkers cross land and water alike, by a third pathfinding of their own over land land units can cross and any water, made the first time one needs it. A hovercraft floats on the water and is hit there as on land, by anything but torpedoes. A seabed walker walks along the bottom: while all of it is under the water it is submerged, as a submarine is, so that only torpedoes reach it and only sonar finds it, and it can fire nothing but torpedoes. The commander and field rigs walk the seabed. A group of land units, amphibians, and ships given one order gets a route for each. The Assault Hovercraft (T2, from T2 and T3 land factories; see T2 Land, Re-derived) is a fast, tough gunboat that hovers. It pays for crossing water: on land about three-quarters as strong for its cost as the Heavy Tank (2,534 health and 145 a shot since 2026-11-25, from 3,129 and 178.8, `[premiums]`). Engineers of every tier hover: they travel on land and float on the water, where they can build naval structures far from the shore. On a map without water this changes nothing: with the unit data's own hash left out and a hovercraft's movement hashed as a land unit's, an AI match's checksum matched the land engineers' on every tick to its end. Under the water a seabed walker cannot fire, so it takes no targets there: on an attack-move it used to stop for one it could never shoot, and a commander stood on the bottom of the sea for the rest of a match.
* **Shields That Move:** A shield may be carried by a unit, and its dome goes with it, on the water for a ship. The Escort Corvette (T2, from naval factories, 700 materiel, 1,500 health) sails with the fleet: a dome of 4,000 health that covers 400 around it on the water and rises 150 above it, flattened beneath the heights attacking aircraft fly at so that their fire must come down through it (a half sphere 400 high until 2026-11-24, which gunships flew inside), for 80 energy a second, and an anti-torpedo charge that stops one torpedo within 600 every 3 seconds (every 6 until 2026-11-24).
* **Anti-Torpedo Charges:** A unit's charges shoot down the nearest enemy torpedo within their range each time they reload, as a tactical missile defense does missiles. The destroyer has an anti-torpedo charge that stops one torpedo within 600 every second (every 10 seconds until 2026-11-24), and the battlecruiser one every 10 seconds. A torpedo bomber drops its five torpedoes of 150 one every 2 seconds, so that a charge stops part of a run and not all of it. It dropped fifteen a run before, from counting each fire's projectiles as more torpedoes.
* **Missiles From Units:** Launchers that store missiles, the tactical and strategic missile launchers and the strategic missile submarine, build one only when told to, or one after another: right-clicking a missile's build button turns that on, marks it "(auto)", and right-clicking again turns it off, as the factory repeat does. They fire what they store only on order. Automatic launchers pick their own targets and need nothing built, and an extra weapon may be one. Both fire from under the water. The Strategic Missile Submarine (T3, from T3 naval factories, 9,500 materiel, 4,000 health) builds and stores up to three nuclear missiles, fired on order out to 8,200, and fires cruise missiles of 2,000 within 60 every 10 seconds on its own, out to 5,120. The Mobile Missile Launcher (T2, from T2 and T3 land factories; see T2 Land, Re-derived) fires cruise missiles on its own, two a volley as one, and cannot hit what is closer than a fifth of its range. A missile flies to where its target was, so a launcher picking its own targets looks over its whole reach, for structures first, then units that have stopped, and only then what is moving. The assault hovercraft moved to the fifth slot of a land factory's bar, on C, as the mobile missile launcher took the artillery slot.
* **Exotic Weaponry:** A weapon's data may give it a stun in seconds. What its shot hits, or its blast reaches, is stunned that long: it cannot move or fire, any of its weapons, its radar, sonar, and deep scan are off, its cloak, stealth, and jamming stop, and its shield dome is down, so shells pass where it stood. A stun on a stunned unit only ever lengthens it. Aircraft are never stunned. A stunned unit, either side's, is marked "STUNNED" while it is in sight. A tractor beam is a weapon kind: it takes hold of one enemy that moves on land at a time, never a commander, an experimental, or a structure, holds it in the air, helpless as if stunned, and draws it in, so that it reaches the holder as its crush time runs out and is destroyed, with the credit to the holder. If the holder dies first, the unit is let go and falls back to the ground. The beam is drawn while it holds. A unit's data may give it a death storm, which it leaves where it is destroyed: the storm drifts toward the nearest enemy within eight times its radius, looking again every second, wanders a random way with none in sight, strikes each enemy under it every tick, never its own side, and blows out when its time is up. It is drawn as a flickering disk.
* **Pause:** The player can stop the game clock with the Pause button beside the match clock, or Ctrl (or Cmd) + P, and start it again the same way. While paused no tick runs, so the AI is paused too, and "PAUSED" shows across the top. The camera, selection, and orders all still work: orders given meanwhile are carried out in order on the first tick after resuming, as if given then. The pause lives in the driver, outside the simulation, so it changes nothing that is replayed or checked. With one human player it is theirs alone; with lockstep multiplayer (§17) it will have to be a command every peer applies on the same tick.
* **Watching Matches:** `--watch` (with `--map` and `--seed`), or `?watch` in the browser, plays the AI-vs-AI match the soak plays for that map and seed, for anyone to watch, with no player. The simulation and both AIs are deterministic, so the map and seed alone replay a soak match tick for tick: no recording is needed, and a test checks the watched match's checksum against the soak's. Matches with a human player need their command log as well, which the game does not save yet. The watcher sees everything, with no fog and both teams' economies in the economy panel, or through one team's eyes (`--view blue`, `?view=red`, or V to step through), with that team's fog, blips, and economy, as its player would. The view and the speed (1x to 16x, - and =) live in the driver, outside the simulation, and can change while paused; neither changes the match. A tap of Space pauses; held, with the mouse, it turns the camera. A watcher selects nothing and gives no orders. When the match ends, a window says who won and offers to watch it again or the next seed; at the soak's limit of 40,000 ticks (60,000, 100 minutes, for team matches of three or more a side and on maps 20 km across or more since 2026-11-25) it says the match was unfinished. In the browser, a frame runs at most 12 ms of ticks when sped up, so slow ticks slow the match instead of the page.
* **AI Tech and Economy:** The AI climbs the land tech tree. Its first land factory upgrades to T2 at 8 materiel a second and to T3 at 30, and its other T1 factories to T2 at 20, one upgrade at a time, and only while its energy income is at least ten times its materiel income, up to 1,500 a second, since an upgrade draws a great deal of energy, and while nothing stalls or its energy store is at least half full: it spends every bit of materiel it makes, so a stall for want of materiel alone never ends, and held a big economy's factories at T2. Once it has a headquarters, new factories are support factories of that tier. A factory first makes engineers of its own tier, up to 2 plus one for every 5 materiel a second and no more than 10, and keeps 3 T2 and 2 T3 engineers once it can build them. Its other units follow its tier: at T1, light bots with every fourth a light artillery; at T2, heavy tanks, with light bots, assault hovercraft, and mobile missile launchers mixed in; at T3, the same with mobile heavy artillery. Generators come before extractors while its energy lags its materiel, and are the only thing it builds while its energy runs out, since extractors cost upkeep. It builds T2 generators from 15 materiel a second and T3 ones from 45, with the engineers that can, while smaller builders help, except while running short of energy slows everything, when every builder makes the best generator it can. Idle builders help finish any structure of 500 materiel or more, then an extractor upgrading, then a factory. From 10 materiel a second, extractors upgrade nearest home first, one at a time plus one for every 25 materiel a second, to T2, and from 40 also to T3, unless energy is short or the team is stalling. Extractors used to pay their upkeep before anything was built, so a team whose generators were all destroyed, and whose income was less than its extractors cost, could never finish another generator: on Twin Passes, raids did that to both sides in one match, which then sat unchanged for 45 minutes. They now share a stall with building (Materiel Extractor Upgrades, above), so a generator always gets built, if slowly. When its energy is gone and its income no more than its running extractors' upkeep, the AI pauses its extractors and moves every builder working on anything else onto generators, and starts the extractors again once energy is half full. Until then it builds only T1 generators, one at a time, with the other builders assisting it: a trickle of energy split between several T2 sites, at 12,000 energy each, finished none of them. Without this, an AI that lost every generator at 15 minutes had none and no energy 10 minutes later; with it, it rebuilt them. Two changes keep both sides of a mirrored match the same: putting generators first keeps the opening energy stall short, where before, with energy empty for minutes, a builder arriving one tick sooner finished its generator 40 ticks sooner, and upgrades wait for an economy that can feed them. With nobody playing Red, the AI has T3 factories and engineers, at least six upgraded extractors, higher-tier generators, and over 60 materiel a second by 25 minutes. Its commander, while idle at home with no enemy near, takes the T2 fabrication pack at 40 materiel a second, the heavy cannon at 60, and the T3 pack at 80, one at a time, never into a stall; earlier, the pack's 21,000 energy held back its climb to T3 and its navy, and starting one while busy threw away its builds, such as a naval factory. An enemy close enough to discharge is worth dropping an enhancement for, and a commander fitting one never joins a wave. From 30 materiel a second it puts field workshops beside its land factories, one plus one for each 40 more and no more than 2. Once it has seen an enemy strategic missile launcher, it builds a strategic missile defense, and keeps it stocked. At 100 materiel and 4,000 energy a second it builds a strategic missile launcher, which keeps building missiles and fires each at the costliest enemy structure in sight, or failing that at the enemy's start. From about 43 minutes in (tick 26,000), any wave of at least twice its first counts as full strength, going for the enemy's base with the commander along: two even sides trading waves of 20 to 40 against each other's defenses otherwise played to the time limit. From 50 minutes (tick 30,000), there are no more waves: everything idle, the commander too, goes straight for the enemy commander, wherever it is, seen or not (the commander itself only if that is on land), since even sides otherwise still traded to the soak's limit now and then, a different seed after each change. Going for where it had last been seen, or else for the enemy's start, two commanders that had each lost their base passed each other under the sea, one going home to rebuild and the other hunting it there, while the armies marched back and forth until the time ran out. Units already on their way are sent again at the enemy commander every 30 seconds, the commander too if it is on an attack-move: sent only once they were idle, a group walked to where it had been while it walked home to rebuild, and was 500 short of it when the time ran out. With an air factory and 35 materiel a second, it keeps one light transport: idle at home, it takes on four to six idle T1 land units, sets them down 250 short of the outlying enemy extractor in sight farthest from the enemy's base (or, with none in sight, the outlying deposit on the enemy's side nearest home), sends them at it, and flies home for the next load. Units boarding are kept out of the army's orders, and if none board within a minute, they go back to it. A transport waiting at home with fewer than four idle T1 units to take has its factories build Light Walkers for it, up to six: once they had moved on to T2, a transport that came late waited for good.
* **AI Air:** The AI builds an air factory once it has a T2 land headquarters or 15 materiel a second, needing no materiel in store for the first, and a second at 50. Air factories upgrade to T2 at 25 materiel a second and to T3 at 60, while its energy income is at least twenty times its materiel income, and once it has an air factory it builds generators to that ratio rather than twelve, since aircraft cost far more energy than land units. While energy is under half full, air factories start nothing. A third of what they make are fighters, the interceptor or at T3 the air superiority fighter, and the rest strike aircraft: the bomber at T1, the gunship and every fourth a fighter-bomber at T2, and the heavy gunship at T3. Against enemy aircraft, two thirds are fighters. Idle fighters patrol between home and the rally point, where they stay in the air and engage whatever comes. Strike aircraft gather at home and set out once there are 6, then 2 more each time up to 16: at the visible enemy nearest home, structures first, or else an outlying deposit or the base, as the land waves choose, and then fly home. With 6 aircraft it builds an airfield. Once it has seen an enemy aircraft, and for 5 minutes after, every fourth land unit is mobile anti-air or, at T2 and above, mobile flak, and its builders put up anti-air toward the enemy, the best each can build: 2, plus one for every 20 materiel a second, up to 6. The land army ignores enemy aircraft, which it cannot hit.
* **AI Navy:** On a map with water, the AI works out once where its naval factories can go: deep water within 7,000 of its start, nearer its start than the enemy's, nearest first, with ties going by the cell as seen from Blue's side so that a mirrored map gets mirrored choices. On Twin Passes the sea's deep water is 5,855 from each start. It also finds the deep water nearest the enemy's start, where its fleets go. It builds a naval factory once it has a T2 land headquarters or 20 materiel a second, needing no materiel in store for the first, and a second at 60, each with a cell of open water around it, out on the water where its engineers float. Naval factories upgrade to T2 at 30 materiel a second once it has 3 ships, and to T3 at 70 once it has 6, so that a factory does not spend its first minutes only upgrading. They build frigates and attack submarines at T1; destroyers, frigates, cruisers, and an escort corvette at T2; and battleships, battlecruisers, destroyers, cruisers, and an escort corvette at T3, and send new ships to wait off the first naval site. Ships and submarines are the fleet's, apart from the land army. Idle ships meet any enemy on or under the water within 7,000 of home, and otherwise set out together once there are 4, then 2 more each time up to 12, for the water nearest the enemy's start, shelling what they find. On a map without water, it plans and builds nothing for the sea.
* **AI Defenses and Intel:** Once the base has two factories, the AI builds a radar toward the enemy, and upgrades it to T2 at 40 materiel a second. With a radar, a blip on the ground within 1,400 of its start draws its idle army, as a seen enemy does; blips more than 30 above the ground or water are aircraft and are left alone. With T2 builders, and energy keeping up with its income, it builds shield generators near home from 20 materiel a second (one more for every 30, up to 4), tactical missile defenses toward the enemy from 20 (one more for every 40, up to 3), and one tactical missile launcher from 25. Planned sites keep their whole footprint clear, not just a corner, which on Red's plan, turned about the map, was the opposite corner and moved its larger buildings two cells off Blue's mirror image. The launcher builds missiles one after another, and fires at enemy structures in sight and in range, extractors first, then the nearest. The commander discharges the costliest enemy unit already in its gun's range, when it has the 7,500 energy in store that a shot needs and is above half health, but never the enemy commander, which takes only 400 from it. To have that much in store, it puts up one energy storage once its income reaches 300 energy a second: built sooner, at 100, it cost Normal enough tempo that Easy's army caught up with it. A commander's reactor takes the other with it when they die close together, and one shot kills a commander: discharging anything in reach drew 7 of 200 soak matches.
* **Upgrade Indicator:** Anything upgrading has the same pulsing ring around it: a commander or field rig being fitted with an enhancement, a factory on an upgrade at the front of its queue that is not paused, and a structure upgrading in place. An enemy's shows too, but only while the unit is in sight, never on a radar blip, and with nothing more: not what it is becoming, nor how far along it is. The viewer's own upgrades also show a progress bar under the health bar. Whatever of the viewer's makes units or missiles (factories, missile silos and defenses, and the strategic missile submarine) shows a blue bar, beneath those, for how far along the unit or missile it is making is, only while it makes one: not while its queue is empty, while it is unfinished or upgrading, or while its silo is full. An enemy's never shows it.
* **Build Menus:** Every build menu, a builder's or a factory's, is one bar. It starts with a T1, T2, T3 toggle, and shows only the items of the level chosen, each as its symbol, drawn large without the map icon's frame and colored by what it is for (orange for factories and builders, green for materiel, yellow for energy, red for direct fire, blue for anti-air, violet for artillery and missiles, teal for torpedoes, periwinkle for shields and missile defenses, and white for intel), with its key in the corner. A builder's bar is grouped, with a divider between groups: factories, then resources (extractors, generators, geothermal plants, storage, and fabricators), then defenses (point defense, anti-air, torpedo launchers, shields, and missile defenses), then intel (radar, sonar, and stealth), then strategic weapons (artillery installations and missile launchers). Items keep their keys whatever their place in the bar. Levels with nothing to build are greyed out. The level chosen is remembered, one for builders and one for factories, and a menu with nothing of that level shows its lowest. Hovering over an item shows, about a sixth of a second after the pointer stops (and at once when moving on from another item), its key and name, its cost and build time, health, and what it does: its weapon, speed, income, upkeep, storage, shield, and sensors, and for factories, how many of those selected can build it. Hotkeys work at any level. A factory's upgrade, repeat, and clear buttons sit on the row below.
* **A Fair Skirmish Map:** The skirmish map turns into itself about its center, and the AI plays it that way. It lays out its base from Blue's side, then turns each site for Red, footprint included. Both commanders start at the center of a terrain cell, never on a cell edge, because a point on an edge belongs to the cell above and to the right of it, so its mirror image would be in the wrong cell. Both AIs think on the same tick, since a side that always thought later would always be that far behind, and they draw the same random numbers. Until the armies meet, Red plays Blue's game turned around. What is left is noise: fixed-point rounding is not symmetric under negation, and some tie-breaks deeper in pathfinding still go by a fixed order (see Mirror Fairness).

## 19. Data & Simulation Requirements

* **Unit Data:** Unit and structure stats live in versioned data files, not code, using fixed-point-safe values and loaded identically on every instance. Each entry defines at least cost (Materiel, Energy, Build Time), health, speed, footprint, movement class, build options, weapons, intel ranges, and resource rates.
* **Economy Precision:** Economy totals, storage, and veterancy XP use 64-bit fixed-point, because their values can exceed the range of the engine's `Fp` type. When demand exceeds supply, resources are shared among consumers using integer math in a fixed order, so stalling stays deterministic.
* **Large Footprints:** Pathfinding for wide units needs cost grids inflated by unit size, in addition to one grid per movement class. Experimentals cannot be added without this.
* **Open Numbers:** Values this document leaves undefined must be set in the data files before their feature is built. Examples include the exact energy-stall build rate, the veterancy regeneration curve, and all T1–T4 unit stats.

## 20. Naming & Licensing

This is a free, open-source game (GPL-3.0), its own game in its genre. Where its ideas came from, and what was replaced and when, is recorded in [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md), the only file that names other games. The rules:

* **Original names and setting.** Factions, units, structures, enhancements, and mechanics have this game's own names (§1.1, §14). A CI check fails on a deny-list of other games' names anywhere but PROVENANCE.md.
* **No borrowed code.** Everything is written for this project. A formula taken from elsewhere is credited where it is used and in PROVENANCE.md.
* **No borrowed content.** No art, models, textures, sounds, maps, scripts, or data files from another game are shipped, committed, downloaded, or read. The bundled maps are generated by this game's own code.
* **Balance from this game's own model.** Balance numbers are derived from this project's own cost model, targets, and testing. Until that rebaseline lands, many of today's values still come from another game's published numbers, listed in PROVENANCE.md, and new values are not taken from it.
