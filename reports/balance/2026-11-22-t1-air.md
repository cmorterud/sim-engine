# T1 air, 2026-11-22

T1 air against T1 land frozen, from rules rather than a solved strength: each rule in data/balance_targets.toml (`[air]`, `[kills]`) sets one number. Anti-air is a hard counter; bombers punish what has none. `--balance-derive t1-air` prints this; `tests/balance_test.rs` checks the unit data against it.

## The rules, and what they set

| Rule | Sets | Value |
|---|---|---|
| A T1 tank takes two bombs (one bomber's two passes, or two bombers' one) | the bomb | 410 (was 350): as big as two allows, since the commander's 18,000 health is fixed by the snipe rules |
| Mobile Anti-Air takes two bombs, at the tank's health per mass | its health and cost | 615 health (was 310), 59 mass and 295 energy (was 55 and 275) |
| One interceptor on a bomber's tail destroys it in about 10 s | the bomber's health | 500 (was 215) |
| An army a fifth anti-air by materiel comes out even with an equal materiel of bombers | Mobile Anti-Air's gun | 20.9 a second (was 26) |
| Fighters turn at least 1.5 times as fast as the T1 bomber | turn rates | T1 bomber 180 degrees a second; Interceptor, Fighter-Bomber, Air Superiority Fighter 270 |
| Flying heights as shares of the spy plane's 440 (`[air.heights]`) | elevations | fighters and heavy bombers 352, air scout 308, T1 bomber and interceptor 264, gunships and transports 198 |

## Mechanics that came with it

- Interceptors' guns are fixed to the nose, firing only within 30 degrees of straight ahead, and a fighter in range behind a slower aircraft eases off to its speed and stays on its tail.
- Anti-air reach counts how far up an aircraft is, and anti-air guns, like flak, miss the more the faster the aircraft and the longer the shell's flight: T1 Mobile Anti-Air hits a T1 bomber 94% of the time, an interceptor 80%, an air scout 62%, and a spy plane next to never. Homing missiles, the SAM's, always hit.
- A bomber that would come round before its bomb is ready flies further out first, and keeps a straight run at its target however tight its turn: no bomber skips a pass.
- Out of fuel, a bomber flies low and slow and still bombs; it used to stop bombing, as it only let go at its full height.
