# Fights between domains, 2026-11-24

Land, air, navy, and the hovercraft, measured against each other for the first time
(BALANCE.md; `[domains]` in `data/balance_targets.toml`). `--balance-derive domains` prints the
tables below. No unit data changed: this is where things stand, before any of it is fixed.

## The principle, and where the numbers come from

Each domain is strongest in its own medium, and the map decides how much of each medium there is.
Navy rules open water and is held back by where water reaches; air is weaker per cost against a
proper fleet, and its strength is reach. A map that is mostly ocean should favor navy; one with
little water leaves it a side show. Each role's strength per cost is set against the inspiration's
ratio (`2026-11-24-inspiration-ratios.md`); the fights are the cells of `[domains.cells]`.

The duels gained two grounds for this (`src/balance.rs`, `Ground`): open water over the whole duel
ground, deep enough for submarines, and a shore, sea in the west and land in the east, where ships
start at sea and positions stand ashore or, for torpedo launchers, on the water off it. Fights at T2
and T3 are priced at their tier (`Field`), both in what each side buys and in what it has left,
since aircraft spend far more energy for their mass than ships do.

## What it says

* **Navy is not a good buy, anywhere.** Every naval role is about half as strong per cost against
  the land as the inspiration has it (0.42 to 0.76 of its ratio), as are the T2 and T3 strike
  aircraft. They kept first-draft prices from before land units were made cheaper for their income
  (2026-11-15), and nothing has re-priced them since.
* **A composed fleet loses to an equal cost of aircraft,** badly: the T2 fleet scores -0.57 against
  the T2 air force (gunships, fighter-bombers, interceptors), and the T3 fleet -0.86 against the T3
  air force, where the target is +0.40 the other way. And it takes only 0.60 times the fleet's cost
  in torpedo bombers to break it.
* **The counters inside the navy are broken:** submarines beat destroyers (-0.80 for the
  destroyers), and hovercraft beat an equal cost of the T2 fleet on the water (+0.43 for the
  hovercraft). What works: a fleet with no anti-air dies to torpedo bombers, submarines sink a fleet
  with no anti-submarine weapon, and destroyers that close on torpedo launchers die.
* **Ships outrange coastal defenses as intended,** cruisers by 200% and battleships by 114%, and
  break a coastal position without loss, but slowly: 146 and 163 seconds against a target of 60,
  since an equal cost buys so few ships.
* **Cruisers against cruisers never finish.** Each carries two missile defenses that shoot down
  every tactical missile the other fires, and they hold at missile range rather than close to their
  guns'. A fleet of cruisers alone cannot fight another.
* **Air against land at T2** comes out at -0.37 for the air force against a land army a fifth
  anti-air: the land army wins, where `[air]` has it even at T1.
* Factories are as the inspiration's, and a naval unit takes much longer to build at its factory
  than anything on land: a destroyer 9.3 times a Medium Tank's time at its factory, a battleship
  17.6, against a gunship's 2.7.

## Open: how hard a fleet should be to break from the air

`torpedo_bombers_to_break_fleet_t2` is [1.5, 3.0] for now: the design's feel, that a T2 fleet with
cruisers and a shield ship is dangerous to anything but overwhelming torpedo bombers. The
inspiration's own numbers say otherwise: about 0.6 to 0.7 times the fleet's cost in torpedo bombers
breaks it, statically and in our duels alike. Torpedo defense, which shoots torpedoes down, is where
the two can meet: ours stops one torpedo every 10 seconds per ship, far too few against a wing of
torpedo bombers. Which way this goes is a design decision to make before the T2 naval pass.

## The AI as a confound

Whole matches measure the AI as much as the units:

* The AI never builds torpedo bombers. Its T2 strike aircraft are gunships, every fourth a
  fighter-bomber, and at T3 heavy gunships, so the air's answer to navy never takes the field.
* Its fleets anchor off the water nearest the enemy's start, and do not seek out coastal targets.
